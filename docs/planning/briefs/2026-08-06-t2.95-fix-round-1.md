## Why this matters more than its size suggests

T2.95 is the mechanism the 2026-08-06 M1 split rests on. The split's licence to merge
implementation years ahead of measurement is one sentence: **no serving path can reach a
label whose evidence does not exist.** The review measured that sentence false on two
doors. Until you close it, every task merged under that licence carries the doubt, and
T2.28 — which mints facts born `corroborated`, the exact claim under gate — is held on
your work specifically.

**The design is not in question and you are not re-opening it.** Enforcement at
`fact_badge.row_labels`, doc 12's one producer, is right; the review says so explicitly.
The defects are that two consumers dodge the producer, one attribute duplicates what the
labels already carry, three schemas drop a field, and two scans enumerate instead of
quantify.

## The six items

1. **Close the two leaks.** `chat.py:401` emits `row.verification_status` — the raw
   ungated `ComposedRow` attribute stamped at `layer_composition.py:951` — while the lines
   either side of it correctly read `row.labels`. It persists into `messages.context` and
   renders in the SPA chat panel. `review_queue.py:336` serves the raw
   `verification_status` on demotion-pending facts. Route both through the producer.
2. **Decide `tier`'s semantics under suppression.** `ComposedRowResponse.tier` serves
   tier 3 beside `labels: candidate`, which leaks the withheld rung numerically — a reader
   or an agent can invert it trivially. The constraint that decides this: **derivation may
   use the true status; the wire may not reveal it.** The module's doctrine explicitly
   permits ranking and `pin_suggestions` to consume promoted statuses internally, so
   ranking's own tier is not the problem — serving it is. Either recompute the served tier
   off the served status, or strip it from the wire and document it as ranking-only. Pick
   one, implement it, and say in your report why the other was worse.
3. **Get `label_suppressed` to the wire.** It exists only in `fact_badge.py` today; every
   schema drops it (`FactListItem`, `FactDetailResponse`, `ComposedRowLabels`, pydantic
   `extra='ignore'`). That makes a suppressed label and an earned `candidate`
   byte-identical on every door — the silent downgrade the task's third Done-when clause
   forbids in as many words. Add it with a **wire-level** test, not a producer-level one:
   the producer already emits it and that is exactly what did not save it.
4. **Rewrite both scans to quantify, not enumerate.** The corroboration scan names two
   files; the grounded scan globs `services/*.py`. The review measured both: a new module
   emitting a promoted literal and a new route emitting `"grounded"` each left the suite
   green. The shape that works is a structural walk of `app/` — **routes and MCP
   included** — asserting the absence of any `verification_status` emission that does not
   originate in `row_labels`. Two notes: write it against the parsed tree, not line text
   (`tests/test_verification_judge.py`'s scan matches the module name as text and fires on
   prose in comments); and your own guard must fail on a path that does not spell the
   name.
5. **Drive at least one REST and one MCP door with the gate closed and no fixture.** The
   `cleared_promotion_record` fixture is what masked the chat leak from the parity test —
   a test given the fixture cannot see a leak. Keep the fixture opt-in and never autouse,
   and add unfixtured door coverage beside it.
6. **Key the gate on `id_kind`.** `served_status` does not, so G-C's promotion record
   would light an article's `grounded` label when T3.02 lands — the wrong record gating
   the wrong claim. Doc 08 §4.3 and doc 09 §4.3 keep those records distinct and you are
   implementing that separation, not deciding it. **Do not import the judge's
   qualification record into `claim_gates.py`** — the single-door scan correctly rejected
   that in the first round, and the module's comment explains why. Where the article gate
   should live is the judge's own door; if closing item 6 properly requires touching that
   module, say so in your report rather than working around the scan.

## How this round is judged

Every item gets a **mutation drill**: break the fix, confirm a named test fails, restore.
Item 4 additionally needs the review's own empirical test re-run — write a new module that
emits a promoted status without going through `row_labels`, and confirm the suite now goes
**red**. A scan that passes its own unit test but not that drill has not been fixed.

The review's drill inventory (its closing section) lists everything it ran, including what
it tried that did **not** break — reproduce the failing ones as regression tests so this
cannot silently re-open.
