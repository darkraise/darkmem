## Global constraints — your attention lens

- **No bar moves.** The 0.01 false-promotion bar and 0.95 confidence are fixed. A
  target *window* is a property of the evidence, not the gate; if the plan blurs
  that anywhere, say so.
- **No validator weakens.** `validate_corroboration_design`,
  `validate_claim_key_convention`, `validate_diversity_group_against_fixture`,
  `validate_arming_design`, `validate_blocked_hold_design` all stand as committed.
- **k must stay exactly 1.** The 473 target is derived at one observed false
  promotion.
- **The plausibility premise is load-bearing**, not decorative: `corpus.json`'s own
  `_notes` say every entry is a capture a working agent could plausibly have made.
- **A measured claim beats an argued one.** Where the plan asserts a mechanism,
  check it against the code and the data rather than against its plausibility.

## What to attack, hardest first

1. **§3.2's mechanism claim is the one I am least sure of, and I am flagging it
   rather than letting you find it flattered.** The plan asserts that a new capture
   carrying a REPLACES-family relation would arm a *second* dispute and move the
   target from 473 to 628. T2.129's arming needed a specific structure — a wave-2
   counterpart re-asserting against a wave-1 subject, with dates outside the
   supersession window. It is entirely possible a stray REPLACES edge produces **no**
   dispute at all, in which case the plan states a false mechanism. This lane has
   already had one fabricated mechanism caught in this exact ledger (the 2-vs-1 hold
   miss); do not let a second stand. Verify against `contradiction_veto`'s
   `inverse_replacement` arm and the ladder, and say plainly whether the rule is
   correct, over-cautious-but-harmless, or wrong.
2. **§3.1's blocker.** Does `validate_diversity_group_against_fixture` really refuse
   two claims naming one dependency, and does the fixture really carry a single repo
   such that +342 diversity claims needs 443 distinct dependencies? Is the
   multi-fixture change actually required, and is it as contained as Step 1 implies?
3. **The 473 itself.** Re-derive it. Is `corroborations_required(1, 0.01, 0.95)`
   473? Is k = 1 the right assumption for a *grown* corpus? Is the unsimulated pass
   the right binding pass, given `time_gap` vanishes without the simulated clock?
4. **The sizing.** 131 unsimulated corroborations from 246 memories; +342 needed;
   conversion ≈ 1 for `diversity`, ≈ 2.7 for `none`; route D at ≈ +615. Recompute.
   The plan already corrected one sizing error (+350 → +615) after the first was
   published in the ledger; a second would be worse than the first.
5. **§3.2's density hazard.** Is accidental supersession actually likely at 2.4–3.5×
   density, or is it a hazard the plan invented? What would it cost if it fired
   undetected, and does Step 3 genuinely catch it?
6. **§6's disclosure.** Is it adequate, or does it soften what a 473-corroboration
   authored corpus can support? Would a future reader quoting "473, bound 0.0100,
   CLEARS" be honestly served by it?
7. **What the plan does not say.** Anything a competent executor would need and
   would not find here.
