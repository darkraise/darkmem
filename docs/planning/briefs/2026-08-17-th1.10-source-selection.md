## What the harvest does, so you can reason about the choice

`span_harvest.py` runs each source through `block_pipeline.ingest_document` —
the **shipping** parser — and exports the resulting blocks to a committed
manifest. That is what makes a premise *real* in TH1.01 §5's sense, and it is
also why the choice of document matters: **the parser decides where the blocks
fall, and the blocks are the premises.** You are choosing block structure, not
just prose.

Parsers by suffix: `.pdf`, `.md` / `.markdown`, `.txt` / `.text`. Verified
behaviour worth knowing before you pick:

- A `.txt` note parses to **one block containing the whole document**. A
  single-block document is nearly useless here — it cannot express a
  cross-block claim, and it makes the two span coordinate systems coincide,
  which is exactly the coincidence that hid a real defect until 2026-08-17.
- A `.md` document parses to one block per heading, paragraph and list item.
  A 10-line markdown file yielded 6 blocks.
- **Prefer markdown.** Include `.txt` and `.pdf` only deliberately, and say why.

## The composition the gold needs, and the arithmetic it implies

The gold needs **≥ 200 pairs, near-misses the majority** (doc 17 §6), spread
over these slices — the four span-specific ones are the point of this gold and
each constrains the documents you must supply:

| Slice | What the document must contain for it to be authorable |
|---|---|
| `span-truncation` | sentences whose supporting clause can be cut mid-way, so a citation can stop before the subject or object |
| `context-dependence` | anaphora across block boundaries — "it", "this approach", a table cell whose meaning needs its header |
| `multi-span-composition` | a claim whose halves sit in **two or more different blocks** |
| `structure-fragment` | lists, tables, and heading-scoped lines whose meaning is partly carried by structure |
| plus the transferable ones | verbatim, paraphrase, weakening, overreach, value-swap, unrelated — ordinary varied prose |

**Derive the document count rather than guessing it, and show the derivation.**
A rough frame: pairs are authored against blocks, a block can carry a small
number of genuinely distinct pairs before they stop being independent evidence
(T2.124's own finding: four pairs of one construction on one premise is
"correlated evidence wearing four rows"), and the gold needs headroom above 200
so retirements do not force a re-harvest. State your target and its slack.

## Two contamination hazards. The second is specific to this step and is easy to walk into.

**1. `CLAUDE.md` auto-injects into your context and carries measured judge
scores.** Every blind author and labeller on T2.124 reported this, and it is
disclosed in that round's commits. It matters less here than for authoring —
you are producing no labels — but state in your record what you were exposed to
rather than claiming a clean room.

**2. Do not select a source document that contains gate results, judge scores,
or bar values.** This repo is full of real markdown, and reaching for
`docs/design/`, `docs/planning/`, `gate-ledger.md` or `CLAUDE.md` is the obvious
move. **It is the wrong move for any document carrying measurement content.**
The premises you commit become the text a judge is later shown, and are read by
whoever authors pairs against them — so a source naming `contradiction_recall
0.7875`, a Clopper-Pearson bound, or a bar value would plant the exact figures
the blind protocol exists to withhold, *inside the gold's own premises*, where
nobody would think to look for them. Screen every candidate and say in your
record how you screened.

Repo docs that carry no measurement content are otherwise good candidates: they
are real, they are ours, and they parse to genuine multi-block structure. Prefer
them over inventing prose to order — doc 17 §6's stance is that synthetic-only
material is unrepresentative — but the screen above overrides that preference
every time.

Also consider, and state your reasoning: third-party documents raise licensing
questions in a repo that will be published, so prefer material this project
owns.
