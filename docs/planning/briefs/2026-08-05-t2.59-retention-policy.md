# Brief — Fable analysis: content retention on delete (T2.59)

**Analysis and options only. This task's own text says it implements whichever policy the
owner states and does not choose one — so you do not choose one either. Write no
implementation.**

**Task:** T2.59 in `docs/planning/tasks/E2-truth-core.md`. Doc 04 §8.1, doc 08 §2.3,
invariant 14. Raised as Tier-3 by T1.29e on 2026-07-27, carried unowned through G-B, and
named by the G-B evidence run (T1.50) as a condition for the controller to discharge.

## The situation

**"Delete my memory" does not remove that memory's content from the system.** It survives
in two stores, not one:

- `events` rows carry a 500-char `payload.content_preview`, and a `buffer_discarded` row
  outlives the memory it came from.
- `messages.context` persists a ~150-char snippet of every cited memory, and nothing prunes
  it on delete.

`events` is **append-only by design** — T1.06's three forbid-triggers, a row-level
UPDATE/DELETE guard plus two TRUNCATE guards. So this is not a missing `DELETE`; it is a
collision between two deliberate properties.

## What to do

Measure the real surface first, because the task text may be stale: confirm both stores
still carry what it says, find any third one it missed, and quantify how much content
actually survives a delete today (which event types, which payload fields, what retention).

Then work the four candidate policies the task names — redact the preview in place (breaks
append-only), stop writing content into events at all (loses triage value for poison rows,
so say what `memory_buffer` triage would lose concretely), tombstone-and-exclude at read
time (content still on disk), accept and document the retention (honest, and possibly
correct for a single-operator homelab) — and for each state what it costs, what it breaks,
what it makes impossible later, and what an operator would actually observe.

Two things to weigh explicitly. This is a **single-operator homelab**, not a
multi-tenant service, and the honest answer may be "accept and document" — say so if you
believe it rather than reaching for the most rigorous-sounding option. And the append-only
journal is load-bearing for audit and undo; a policy that breaks it must argue why the
audit trail is worth less than the deletion guarantee.

## Deliver

Write to `docs/planning/analyses/2026-08-05-t2.59-retention.md`. Lead with the measured
surface, then the option table, then a recommendation clearly flagged as the controller's
decision to make.
