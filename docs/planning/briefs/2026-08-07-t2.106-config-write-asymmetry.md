**Task:** T2.106 · `Sonnet 5` · `high` · `Size: S` · Doc 11 §6, doc 12 §2 · Gate: standing

## The state you inherit

T2.104 fixed a silent drop: `LLMConfigResponse` (`backend/app/schemas/config.py:45`)
declared six purposes while both `/config/llm` routes always built all eight, so
pydantic's default `extra="ignore"` discarded `synthesis` and `verification` with no
error. It now declares all eight, and an operator can finally *see* them.

`LLMConfigUpdate` (same file, ~line 60) still declares six. So neither purpose can be
*changed* through the route that displays it. T2.104 left that deliberately, on
instruction, because widening a write is a wire-contract decision rather than a
bookkeeping fix.

**The half-state is an improvement, not a resting place.** A settings surface that
displays a value it cannot edit invites the reasonable inference that the value is not
editable at all — when in fact it is simply unreachable from this door.

## This is a decision task, and the decision is not symmetric

**Do not close the asymmetry by symmetry.** The two purposes differ in the one way that
matters:

- **`synthesis` is seeded inert.** Doc 11 §6 makes it resolvable from day one; nothing
  dispatches on it until T3.02. Widening the write is cheap and its blast radius is a row
  nobody reads yet.
- **`verification` is live.** `app/services/verification_judge.py` resolves and dispatches
  on it (since T2.26), and `VerificationJudge.judge()` refuses unconditionally without a
  qualification record.

That second point is the whole task. **A judge qualified on one model and then silently
repointed to another has a qualification record that no longer describes what runs** —
and the qualification is G-D's gate evidence (T2.41, on the entailment gold set T2.54).
Letting an operator repoint a live judge through a settings form is a real capability
with a real failure mode, and it reaches into a gate.

So: rule per purpose, and record the reasoning. **"Read-only for `verification`, and here
is why" is a complete answer** and may well be the right one. If you do make it writable,
the interaction with the qualification record has to be stated and guarded — a write that
invalidates a qualification should say so, not silently strand it.

Read the gate ledger's G-D section before you decide; it is where the qualification's
bars live, and it will tell you what a stale qualification would cost.

## What makes each clause non-vacuous

- [ ] **The asymmetry is resolved deliberately per purpose, with the reasoning
      recorded** — not closed by symmetry alone. A report that says "made both writable
      for consistency" has not done this task.
- [ ] **If `verification` becomes writable, the interaction with its qualification record
      is stated and guarded; if it stays read-only, the UI says so rather than appearing
      broken.** The second half is the one most likely to be skipped: a field rendered
      identically to editable siblings but silently rejected on save is worse than a
      field marked read-only. Whatever you rule, the surface must not lie about it.
- [ ] **T2.104's guard still passes, and any new asymmetry it would catch is caught.**
      The guard (`backend/tests/response_coverage.py`) covers `facts_read` and the
      `/config/llm` GET. If your change introduces a request-side shape the guard cannot
      see, say so — T2.104 explicitly recorded that it does not cover the ~18 other
      `response_model=` routes, and inheriting that limit knowingly is fine; inheriting it
      silently is not.

## Scope boundaries

- **Do not widen the guard's coverage to other routes.** That is deliberately out of
  T2.104's scope and remains out of yours; if you think it deserves a task, say so.
- **Do not change what any purpose does, or touch the judge.** You are deciding a wire
  contract, not verification behaviour.
- **Do not alter the seeded values** in `app/services/llm_seed.py`.
- **No schema migration** — these are pydantic models and `llm_purpose_configs` rows.
- **Check `docs/api-rest-conventions.md`** before changing a request payload.
