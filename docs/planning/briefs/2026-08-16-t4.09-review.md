## The lens

Two verdicts, per runbook §6: **spec compliance** against the task's Done-when
("every assumption listed with its owning code path and the concrete failure a
second worker causes; written up as a decision input, not a plan"), and **task
quality**.

Press hardest on these, because they are where this repo's audits have failed
before:

- **Is the audit itself complete?** A completeness audit that was itself
  incomplete has already been caught once in this repo. The audit found four
  hazards the corpus never named — so the corpus is not a bound on the true set,
  and neither is the audit. Look for a fifth. An enumeration whose count is its
  headline is exactly the "here is the full list" shape invariant 23 exists for,
  and stale or short counts have gone undetected here through four landings.
- **Are the two ALREADY-SAFE exonerations right?** Saying an assumption does not
  break is the highest-consequence claim in the document, because it is the one
  T4.10 will rely on to *not* act. Verify both at the tree, including whether
  "inert today" is being used to mean "safe" — the PPR arm is inert only while
  `w_ppr = 0.0`, and T4.10 may well be deciding for a future where it is not.
- **Does it stay a decision input rather than becoming a plan?** The Done-when
  draws that line explicitly.
- **Are the BREAKS/DEGRADES/ALREADY-SAFE assignments defensible one by one**, or
  does the boundary between DEGRADES and BREAKS move to suit the count?
- **Check the negative claims by construction**, not by grep alone — "no lease
  exists anywhere in `backend/app`" and "zero `pg_advisory_lock`" are the kind of
  claim a single grep can appear to confirm while missing a synonym.

Do not fix anything. Return findings and the two verdicts. If you conclude a
finding is the owner's call rather than the implementer's, say so and say why.
