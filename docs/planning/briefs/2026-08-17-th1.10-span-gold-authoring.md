# The claim/span faithfulness gold — what the owner must author for G-F

**Owner ruling, 2026-08-17:** the owner authors this gold. The loop may not
(`autonomous-loop.md` §7 names all five gold sets and forbids writing any of
them), so G-F stays open until this file exists. Everything else on G-F's path
is built: `TH1.01` defined the metric and committed the bars before any build,
`TH1.17` shipped the runner and scorer and has passed both an adversarial review
and an independent fix-round re-review, and `TH1.09` — the runtime span-support
check — is `done`. **The gold is the only missing input.**

This brief is the operational form of `TH1.01` §5. It states what to produce,
where, in what shape, and how to check a draft without spending a measurement
attempt. It adds no rule; where it looks like it does, the spec governs:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-15-faithfulness-metric-and-bar-design.md`.

## AMENDED 2026-08-17 — the premises are real, not hand-authored

**Owner ruling, same day: use real block spans and real QA drafts.** This brief
was first written against TH1.01 §5 as it stood, where `document-span` and
`qa-draft` were *reserved at zero pairs* "until their producer task exists" and
`hand-authored-fixture-span` was the only permitted provenance. TH1.02 and TH1.08
both shipped in v1.0, so the producers exist — and doc 17 §6's recorded stance is
that this gold is seeded from real block answers, with **synthetic-only entailment
gold rejected as unrepresentative**. Authoring 200 fixture pairs would therefore
have produced exactly the gold that stance rejects, and you would have found that
out after the work rather than before.

What changed in the tree, so this brief is executable rather than aspirational:

- **The reservation is lifted.** `RESERVED_ZERO_PROVENANCE` is now empty and both
  real provenances load. The test that asserted each was refused was *inverted*
  rather than deleted — emptying the set would have left it parametrized over
  nothing, green while asserting nothing.
- **There is a harvester**: `backend/scripts/benchlib/span_harvest.py`. It ingests
  committed source documents through `block_pipeline.ingest_document` — the
  shipping parser, which is what makes a premise *real* in §5's sense — and
  exports the resulting blocks to a committed manifest. `--check` re-harvests and
  fails if the manifest would change, which is also a parser-determinism check.
- **`slice_tags` is now validated.** It never was; see the amended warning below.

**Two coordinate systems, and this is the one thing most likely to silently ruin
the gold.** `blocks.char_span` records where a block sits in the *source file*.
A citation's `char_span` indexes into the *block's own text*, from 0 — because
`faithfulness_gate._entails` puts `block.text[start:end]` to the judge. The
harvest manifest therefore names the first `source_char_span` (traceability only)
and gives you `text` plus `text_length` to author against. **Never copy
`source_char_span` into a pair.**

> **RULED AND IMPLEMENTED 2026-08-17 — one basis, the runtime's.** The
> inconsistency escalated here (the fixture path sliced the *document file* while
> the runtime slices the *block's text*) is closed, and closed by unification
> rather than by labelling two bases: keeping both is what let the divergence hide
> in the first place.
>
> An anchor is now `{document_uri, block_key, text_sha256, char_span}` and
> resolves as `manifest_block.text[start:end]` — byte-for-byte what
> `app.services.faithfulness_gate._entails` puts to the judge. A retired
> `fixture_path` anchor is **refused** rather than silently loaded, because it
> cannot be resolved faithfully. `text_sha256` is the byte-bind and catches a
> source edit *and* a parser change, either of which moves a premise out from
> under a pair already labelled against it.
>
> A fixture document is no longer a special case — it is a harvested source like
> any other, which is why `tests/fixtures/faithfulness_gate/manifest.json` now
> exists. Why the old basis appeared to work: both `.txt` fixtures parse to a
> **single** block whose text is the whole document, so document offsets and block
> offsets coincided. `doc_pipeline.md` was added to stop that coincidence covering
> the divergence — it parses to six blocks, its `b1` sits at source `[20, 89]`
> while its own text runs `0..69`, and `fx-9` cites two *different* blocks. A
> mutation that regresses to document coordinates now reds four tests; before the
> multi-block fixture existed, it reded none.

## What the gold is for

`TH1.10` qualifies the verification judge for the **faithfulness role** — a
third role beside `entailment` and `contradiction`, and its own role on purpose:
the discrimination is the same word ("is this claim entailed?") over a different
premise distribution. Article claims are put against L2 fact text; faithfulness
claims are put against **raw document span bytes**, which the claim/fact gold
does not represent. So the entailment record qualifies articles and not this,
and a separate gold is what makes the separation real rather than asserted.

The metric being qualified is **span-support coverage** = entailed answer-claims
÷ total answer-claims, per answer. Three properties of that metric decide what
the gold must contain:

- **The claim unit is the answer sentence.** No sub-sentence splitting.
- **The premise is the cited span set, verbatim** — the ordered concatenation of
  exactly those span bytes. The judge sees no surrounding block, no document
  fallback, no search-for-support.
- **Whole-hypothesis scoring.** A conjunction with one unsupported conjunct is
  not entailed. Partial credit does not exist.

## Where it goes

| Thing | Path |
|---|---|
| The gold file | `backend/scripts/benchlib/entailment/span_gold.json` |
| Fixture documents it anchors into | the same directory (any layout you choose beneath it) |
| Its labelling rules | a sibling `LABELLING.md` for the span gold — **not** the claim/fact one |
| Judge output quarantine | `backend/scripts/benchlib/entailment/cache_span/` (already reserved) |

It is a **sibling file**, never merged into `gold.json`. The claim/fact gold's
`LABELLING.md` says so itself. The runner already looks at exactly this path and
raises `SpanGoldAbsent` rather than scoring an empty set as a pass, so the file
appearing is the whole trigger.

## The exact shape the loader accepts

`load_span_gold` refuses a document with no `pairs` key, a pair with no usable
`id`, a duplicate `id`, and a pair missing `answer_id` or `hypothesis`.
`validate_pair_shape` refuses a bad verdict, a bad provenance, a reserved
provenance, and an empty `premise_spans`. One pair:

```json
{
  "id": "sg-0001",
  "answer_id": "ans-mvcc-01",
  "hypothesis": "Postgres uses MVCC for concurrency control.",
  "verdict": "entailed",
  "provenance": "document-span",
  "slice_tags": ["verbatim"],
  "rationale": "General procedure 1: the span asserts the claim in the same words.",
  "premise_spans": [
    {
      "document_uri": "mvcc.md",
      "block_key": "b1",
      "text_sha256": "<the manifest block's text_sha256, copied verbatim>",
      "char_span": [0, 43],
      "text": "Postgres uses MVCC for concurrency control."
    }
  ]
}
```

`document_uri` and `block_key` name a block in the harvest manifest;
`char_span` indexes **into that block's `text`, from 0**, bounded by its
`text_length`. Copy `text_sha256` from the manifest — it is the byte-bind, and a
mismatch is `SpanAnchorDrift` rather than a wrong premise served quietly.

Four things the loader enforces that are easy to get wrong:

1. **`verdict` is binary — `entailed` or `not-entailed`.** There is no
   `contradicted` here, deliberately: no consumer makes a contradiction
   discrimination over spans, and a `contradicted` census would mint numbers no
   role's gate reads. Hard negatives (value swaps, reversals) are still
   authored; they are labelled `not-entailed` and carried by their slice.
2. **`provenance` is now `document-span` or `qa-draft` for real material**, per
   the 2026-08-17 ruling — the reservation is lifted and the loader accepts both.
   `hand-authored-fixture-span` still loads and is the right value for a pair
   built on a committed fixture rather than parser output; what it must no longer
   be is the *whole* gold. Use `document-span` when the premise came out of the
   harvest manifest, and `qa-draft` when the hypothesis was harvested from a real
   TH1.08 answer.
3. **`premise_spans` is an ordered list**, not a string. A pair may cite several
   spans; the premise is their concatenation joined by a blank line, in the order
   given. This is the whole reason the claim/fact schema could not be reused.
4. **`text_sha256` byte-binds the anchor.** `resolve_anchor_text` looks the block
   up in the manifest, re-hashes its text, and raises `SpanAnchorDrift` on any
   mismatch — and also when the block is no longer in the manifest at all. So a
   changed source *or* a changed parser fails loudly and forces
   retire-and-replace, exactly as corpus drift does for `l2-fact` premises.
   **Do not type hashes or offsets by hand.** Write them with a script that
   locates the substring inside the anchored block's text and copies the
   manifest's own hash; the fixture builder
   (`backend/scripts/benchlib/tests/fixtures/faithfulness_gate_fixture.py`) shows
   the pattern and exists precisely so a hand-typed offset can never silently
   disagree with the text it claims to cite.

`char_span` bounds are checked against the **block's** text, which is the
coordinate system a citation uses. An empty range (`end == start`) is deliberately
*not* refused — that is a known separate defect in `_span_literal`'s storage
(TH1.04) and is yours to fix, not the loader's.

## Composition

- **≥ 200 pairs** (doc 17 §6), **near-misses the majority.** A gold of easy pairs
  measures nothing: the judge's job is the boundary, so the boundary is where the
  pairs must sit.
- **One slice per pair**, carried in `slice_tags` (a **list** of strings, even
  when it holds one), and a `rationale` citing a named rule from your
  `LABELLING.md` — not a free-text justification.

  > **This is now validated — it was not when this brief was first written.**
  > `validate_pair_shape` had checked the verdict, the provenance, the ids and
  > the spans, and checked `slice_tags` not at all, while
  > `score_span_judgments` read `pair.get("slice_tags", [])`. So a pair with the
  > field **missing entirely scored into the aggregate and into no slice** —
  > invisible in every per-slice number while still moving the headline, which
  > is ledger rule 4's collapse arriving through a hole rather than a decision.
  > A bare string was the subtle case: `for tag in "verbatim"` iterates
  > characters, minting eight single-letter phantom slices. The loader now
  > requires a non-empty list of non-blank strings, and all three guards are
  > mutation-drilled. **The vocabulary itself is still yours** — the loader
  > checks shape, not membership, so a typo'd-but-well-formed tag still makes a
  > slice of one. Pin your vocabulary in `LABELLING.md` and check the per-slice
  > counts against the pair count before certifying.
- **Four span-specific slices** the claim/fact gold has no analogue for, and they
  are the point of this gold rather than decoration:
  - `span-truncation` — the citation cuts off before (or starts after) the clause
    that would support the claim. The surrounding block supports it; the citation
    does not. Verdict `not-entailed`. This is the failure a span-anchored
    citation system uniquely invents.
  - `context-dependence` — the span supports the claim only through an antecedent
    outside it ("it", "this approach", a table cell without its header). Verdict
    `not-entailed`, by the judge-sees-only-the-spans rule. The slice exists to
    catch a judge that resolves anaphora from world plausibility.
  - `multi-span-composition` — the claim is entailed only by the conjunction of
    two or more cited spans, no single one sufficing. Verdict `entailed`. The
    recall-side hard case for a judge that reads only the first span.
  - `structure-fragment` — the span is a list item, table fragment, or
    heading-scoped line whose meaning depends on document structure the span text
    carries only partially. Mixed verdicts, decided by what the bytes alone
    assert.
- **Plus the transferable slices**, reinterpreted over document prose: verbatim,
  paraphrase, weakening, overreach, value-swap, unrelated.
- **The claim/fact temporal slice does not transfer** — block spans carry no
  validity windows. Temporal material appears only as ordinary prose content.

## The procedure, in the order it must happen

1. **Write `LABELLING.md` first, rule-then-pairs.** The rules exist before any
   pair does. This is not ceremony: T2.124 spent most of its length discovering
   that a rule written to settle a pair already in front of you is a fitted rule,
   and the ordering is what stops that. Reuse the claim/fact gold's discipline
   wholesale — append-only with declared counts, retire-in-place never delete,
   provenance vocabularies pinned by test, proposition-level (not byte-level)
   independence.
2. **Choose and commit the source documents.** Real documents, varied structure —
   you need headings, lists and a table for `structure-fragment` to be authorable
   at all, and the parsers take `.pdf`, `.md`/`.markdown` and `.txt`/`.text`.
   **Which documents you pick shapes the gold's distribution, so the choice is
   yours, not the harvester's.** Put them under
   `backend/scripts/benchlib/entailment/span_sources/`.
3. **Harvest the premises.** This is mechanical and mine to have built:

   ```
   cd backend && python scripts/benchlib/span_harvest.py \
       --sources scripts/benchlib/entailment/span_sources \
       --out scripts/benchlib/entailment/span_harvest.json
   ```

   Commit the manifest. `--check` re-harvests and fails if it would change, so a
   changed source or a changed parser is caught rather than silently invalidating
   every pair anchored into it.
4. **Harvest QA drafts** for the `qa-draft` hypotheses — real TH1.08 answers.
   Note this needs no qualified judge: generating an answer is the flow's job,
   and only the faithfulness *gate* needs the judge, so drafts are producible
   now. Do not run the gate over them while authoring.
5. **Author the pairs**, blind to any judge output, against the manifest's `text`
   and `text_length`.
6. **Two independent blind labelling passes**, with owner escalation on
   disagreement. Judge output (`cache_span/`, any `verdicts/` sibling) is
   quarantined from every author and adjudicator.
7. **Then, and only then, the measurement.** `TH1.10` runs it once.

## Two contamination warnings, both learned the expensive way

**CLAUDE.md auto-injects into every agent's context, and it carries measured
judge scores.** T2.124 disclosed this: every blind author and labeller on that
round reported it. CLAUDE.md states the four contradiction-recall figures and the
bar values verbatim, so any agent you use for *any* part of this — even
mechanical work like computing hashes — is not blind in the sense the protocol
means. Keep agents off the authoring and labelling passes, or state precisely
what each one saw.

**Do not look at a faithfulness number before the gold is certified.** The bars
are already committed (below) and were derived, not fitted, before any build. The
value of that ordering is destroyed by one glance at a result followed by one
edit to a pair.

## The bars this will be scored against — already committed, not yours to set now

In `backend/app/services/faithfulness_bars.py`, landed by TH1.01 before any of
this existed:

| Bar | Value | What it gates |
|---|---|---|
| `faithfulness_gate_precision_bar` | 0.95 | the faithfulness role's qualification, truth-critical side |
| `faithfulness_gate_recall_bar` | 0.90 | the same qualification, other side |
| `faithfulness_coverage_bar` | 1.0 | the per-answer serving boundary — the grounded label asserts *full* grounding, so it is length-invariant by design |

All three are **floors** under the ladder's clamp shape, with a per-parameter
conservative direction and a `faithfulness_bar_licences` row. Tightening is free;
loosening needs a recorded argument, and none may move to make a run pass.

## How to check a draft without spending anything

```
cd backend && python scripts/benchlib/run_faithfulness_gate.py
```

With no `--live` this loads and shape-validates the gold, reports the pair count
and prints the committed/effective/status line for every bar. It makes **no model
call** — scoring is opt-in behind `--live` *and* `--api-key`, and the runner
fails closed rather than probing when either is missing. Run it after every
authoring session; it will catch a duplicate id, a drifted hash, a missing
`answer_id` or `hypothesis`, a bad verdict or provenance, and an out-of-bounds
span immediately, and none of that costs an attempt.

Two things it will **not** catch: a wrong or missing `slice_tags` value (above),
and whether a label is *right*. The second is what the two blind passes are for.

Note the runner also reports, in its own words, that it does not check for a
qualification record — so a present, scored gold clearing its bars is not yet the
same as a licensed serving decision. `TH1.10` is what turns one into the other.
