## Claims to attack rather than accept

1. **The window is 473, not 299.** 299 is T2.130's k = 0 window; this corpus has
   k = 1 because T2.129 armed the deterministic detector, and the derived window
   for k = 1 is 473. The note states that at n = 300 the 95% Clopper-Pearson
   bound is 0.0157 — over half again the 0.01 bar — so no verdict moves.
   Recompute this with the committed instrument rather than reading it.
2. **The corpus is 264 claims. Does 264 reach 473 in-window corroborations?**
   This is the arithmetic that decides whether the run is worth authorising, and
   it is the one a reader is most likely to assume rather than check. Claims are
   not corroborations. Derive the expected in-window count from the committed
   corpus and say whether it clears, and with what margin.
3. **The 0.9703 conversion rate was kept rather than improved.** Three
   unconverged claims were identified — `elasticsearch`, `redis` (one decidable
   predicate: `code_deriver._relation_type_for` asserts `USES` where the memory
   lifts `DEPENDS_ON`) and `requests` (authoring drift). The note says screening
   all 101 committed imports returns *exactly* those two, so the shortfall is
   the whole population rather than a sample of a rate — and that new claims
   should therefore convert at or above 0.9703, but the sizing was deliberately
   not re-banked on that. Check the screen really is exhaustive, and whether
   holding the rate is conservative or merely unexamined.
4. **The embedder merge screen.** 15 name pairs sit at or above Phase 4's 0.85
   with no discriminating token; four were replaced and the count held at
   exactly 264. But `bcrypt`↔`cryptography` at 0.8618 are both existing claims
   and both converged, i.e. a mergeable-looking pair did not merge — the screen
   over-predicts near the threshold. Does that over-prediction mean the four
   replacements were unnecessary, and does replacing them cost anything?
   Separately: `atlas-ingest`↔`atlas-query` at 0.8582 is recorded as a latent
   hazard left untouched, "harmless only because no two atlas claims share an
   object name". Verify that condition holds in the committed corpus.
5. **T2.132 changed this task's standing mid-flight.** The targets were derived
   from the Clopper-Pearson bound while the gate still scored the point
   estimate; the gate now applies the bound, so reaching the window and clearing
   the audit are one event rather than two. Confirm the corpus was not sized
   against the retired rule anywhere that still matters.
6. **The corpus is authored end to end by the measured party.** That limit is
   already recorded in `CLAUDE.md` beside the number. Say whether anything in
   this corpus makes it worse than the previous one on that axis.
