# T2.131 step-level plan — adversarial Fable-tier review (2026-08-15)

**Artifact:** `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-15-t2.131-corpus-growth-to-473.md`, reviewed
before execution per runbook §3 (Size L). Reviewed against T2.131's task text, the gate

(`promotion_corpus.py`, `promotion_metrics.py`, `contradiction_veto.py`, `fact_lift.py`,

## Verdicts

**Spec compliance: ❌ as written.** The plan targets the right number on the right pass
and maps its steps onto most of the Done-when, but executed literally it cannot discharge
it: Step 1 modifies `validate_diversity_group_against_fixture` while the Done-when says
"every existing validator passes unmodified", and the conflict is nowhere named (I4); the
Done-when's "growth is spread across projects" is unachievable on the diversity arm the
recommended route leans on, because code-derived facts are pinned to project `default`
(C2); and the Done-when's echo/under-sourced controls check appears in no step (M3). None
of these is fatal to the route — each has a resolution — but the plan does not contain
them, and a reviewer passing it as compliant would be signing the letter of a Done-when
the plan's own Step 1 breaks.

**Plan quality: REWORK.** Two findings meet the brief's Critical bar ("a claim the code
does not support"). The derivation core survives intact — 473 is correct, +342 is
correct, the two blockers in §3 are real, route D's arithmetic is internally consistent
and the step skeleton (batches, reconciliation, both passes, independent verdict) is
sound. What needs rewriting is the mechanism §3.2 rests its first rule on, Step 1's
multi-fixture design, §4's spread rationale, and §7's catch column — all against the code
rather than against plausibility — plus a conversion margin in the sizing.

## Critical

### C1 — §3.2's first rule stands on a mechanism the code refutes (the brief's item 1)

The plan: *"No new capture may carry a REPLACES-family relation … One that does can arm
`inverse_replacement`, and a second dispute moves the target from 473 to 628 — the growth
would outrun its own goal."*

**The rule is right. The stated mechanism is wrong.** Three independent facts, each
verified in the code:

1. **A stray REPLACES edge arms nothing by itself.** `detect_open_contradiction`
   (`contradiction_veto.py:192-202, 340-361`) fires only when a REPLACES-family *fact*
   has a live inverse *fact* — subject and object swapped — in the **same project**, with
   overlapping belief windows, whose counterpart carries an attributable origin the
   subject lacks. One direction alone produces no finding. And the only existing
   REPLACES-family facts live in `warehouse` and `scheduler`, where the intruder clauses
   (`promotion_corpus.py:631-641, 809-821`) refuse any growth entry.
2. **Even an accidental inverse pair cannot move k.** The audit's numerator counts only
   facts journaled `corroborated` *before* the dispute
   (`promotion_metrics.py:465-491`; `_ARMED_DISPUTES`' `corroborated_first`). The loader
   refuses wave 2 outside the arming groups (`promotion_corpus.py:412-418`), so any
   accidental pair among growth captures lands whole in wave 1, where every fact and
   provenance row exists before the first recompute tick — and `decide` checks the veto
   ahead of the count, so the accidental subject goes `candidate → disputed` with no
   corroboration in between. That is T2.129's own founding observation, quoted in the
   plan's source module (`promotion_corpus.py:70-87`), applied back at it. **k = 2 is
   unreachable in any corpus the committed loader admits; reaching it requires weakening
   a validator**, which the constraints forbid. The 473→628 jump cannot happen by
   accident.
3. **What a stray REPLACES edge actually does** is the plan's *second* bullet: at ingest,
   `detect_supersessions` retires same-project active memories whose `technologies`
   overlap the targets inside the 90-day window — pass 1's failure shape — and the
   supersession hold then withholds the promotion, shrinking the denominator. That
   hazard is real, and it — not a second dispute — is the whole reason rule 1 earns its
   place. It can also cost the pair's own corroboration (a dispute out of `candidate`
   removes an expected promotion).

Answering the brief's direct question: **the rule is correct to keep, over-cautious in
effect, and the mechanism given for it is wrong** — an un-derived, plausible-sounding
claim of exactly the genus this ledger has recorded twice (the pass-2 batch-boundary
story, the 2-vs-1 hold miss). The fix is one paragraph: keep rule 1, motivate it by
supersession and by dispute-census noise, and state positively that the 628 scenario is
validator-fenced. (One theoretical residue: a wave-1 lift group that stalls and completes
during the wave-2 lift could in principle late-create a fact, but a stalled group writes
nothing by definition and the count is recorded — `harvest_l2_facts.py:150-172`; not a
live path.)

### C2 — Step 1 and route B claim a project knob that does not exist

Step 1: *"Widen the fixture loader to carry several repos with their own ids and
projects."* Route B's verdict: *"spreads projects, which also answers the monoculture
concern."*

**Repositories carry no project, and code-derived corroboration is pinned to project
`default`.** `CODE_FACT_PROJECT = "default"` (`fact_lift.py:647-657`), used by the code
lift's candidate match (`fact_lift.py:741, 1081`), the born-corroborated mint
(`fact_lift.py:988`) and the code-source journal (`fact_lift.py:942`). `triple_key`
embeds the project, so a diversity memory captured in any other project lifts a
*different* fact that the repository's assertion never seconds — the claim silently holds
at one origin, exactly the silent-shrink the diversity validator's docstring warns about,
and **no committed validator reads a diversity entry's project**, so nothing refuses it.
This is not incidental: all 101 existing diversity memories sit in `default` because they
must (verified against `corpus.json`).

Consequences the plan does not carry:

- Route B/D's diversity arm **cannot spread projects** without changing shipping app
  code — a documented design decision (`fact_lift.py:647-656` states the under-claiming
  rationale) that a bench task has no business flipping, and that the plan nowhere
  budgets or even names. Multi-repo diversity works, but inside `default`: distinct repo
  names give distinct fact subjects and distinct code origins, which is what makes
  per-repo dependency dedup sound.
- The monoculture answer must come from the `none` arm alone; §4's route comparison
  should be re-argued with that constraint (B's edge over C narrows to plausibility of
  the fixtures; C's edge is the only project spread available).
- Route D's +205 diversity captures all land in `default`, taking it from 101 to ~306
  memories — the plan's own density concern is most acute in exactly the project its
  spread premise thought it was relieving.
- An executor following Step 1 as written would either build a repo-project knob that
  has nowhere to plug in, or author project-spread diversity claims whose bucket shrinks
  with no error until step 4's count comes up short.

## Important

### I1 — §2's conversion table misstates two of its three measured figures

The table conflates **gate buckets** with **corpus groups**:

- *"131 unsimulated corroborations (buckets: diversity 98, none 35)"* — 98 + 35 = 133 ≠
  131. The 35 is the `none` bucket's *eligible* count (33 promoted + 2 hold events), and
  the 33 promoted include the armed subject's fact; the `none` **group** contributes 32.
  The correct split is 98 + 33.
- `none` at *"≈ 2.7 memories per corroboration"* — the marginal design cost is exactly
  **3.0** (N = 3 met exactly, three distinct origins, one memory each: 96 memories → 32
  claims). The 2.7 is 96 ÷ 35 — the group's memories over a bucket containing two other
  groups' facts.
- `time_gap` at *"≈ 1.8"* — it is 2.0 (36 memories, 18 claims). Moot for growth, but the
  same error a third time.

§4's route arithmetic quietly uses the correct 3.0 (410 ≈ 137 × 3; 1030 ≈ 342 × 3), so
the bottom line survives — but this is the table the sizing stands on, in a plan whose
reason for existing includes its author's previous sizing error. Taking "≈ 2.7" literally
prices route C at ~920 captures, not ~1030.

### I2 — The sizing carries no conversion margin, and route D lands short in expectation

Diversity's measured conversion is 98/101 ≈ 0.97, which the task text itself flags
("converts *near* one-for-one"). Route D's +205 diversity claims therefore expect ~199
corroborations, and 199 + 137 + 131 ≈ **467 < 473** — the plan's recommended route, sized
exactly, misses its own target, discovered only at step 4. Author a margin (~+10–15
diversity claims or equivalent), and prefer overshooting for a second reason: at exactly
n = 473 the bound is 0.009990, which every renderer in this lane prints as **0.0100** —
visually equal to the bar under a strict `<` rule. At n ≈ 490 the bound reads 0.0096 and
the "does 0.0100 < 0.0100?" question a future quoter would trip on never arises.

### I3 — §7's first catch does not exist as described

*"Step 2 validator gate; step 3 asserts k == 1 before the run."* No committed validator
inspects a non-arming capture for replacement content — `_declared_replacement` runs only
for the arming and blocked-hold groups — and the live leak path is the **extractor** (and
the rescue layer, which mints REPLACES from "instead of" / "builds on" phrasing at
0.7–0.75 confidence), which no corpus-side validator can see. "Asserts k == 1 before the
run" is incoherent: k is a run outcome; what is assertable before the run is that the
corpus arms exactly one pair, which `validate_arming_design` already does. The real
catches are run-time — `_SUPERSEDED_MEMORIES` per project, the dispute census,
step 3's authored-vs-counted reconciliation — and the plan should say so, because as
written an executor believes a guard exists that does not, and a content-level authoring
check (refuse replacement phrasing in growth content) would be a cheap real addition.

### I4 — Step 1 modifies a validator the Done-when says passes unmodified

Making `validate_diversity_group_against_fixture` per-repo is a *modification* and,
literally, a *widening* (two claims naming one dependency become admissible when seconded
by different repos). It is defensible — with distinct repo subjects the claims converge on
distinct facts, so the invariant's substance is preserved — but T2.131's Done-when says
"every existing validator passes unmodified and the diff shows no validator weakened",
and the global constraints say the diversity validator "stands as committed". The plan
must name this conflict and have it adjudicated (letter vs intent) **before** execution,
not discover it at review. The per-repo variant also needs one property the plan's
one-liner does not state: each claim must bind to exactly **one** repo (content names the
subject repo, as every existing entry does — "payments-gateway depends on httpx…"), and
ambiguity across repos importing the same dependency must be refused the way ambiguity
across dependencies is today.

## Minor

- **M2.** The density hazard is framed as a function of density; it is a function of
  REPLACES-emitting captures × same-project in-window candidates. Under rule 1 it is

## The brief's seven attacks, answered in order

1. **§3.2's mechanism** — the rule is right, the mechanism is wrong (C1). A stray
   REPLACES edge produces no dispute; an accidental inverse pair produces disputes out of
   `candidate` that never enter the numerator; k = 2 is unreachable without a validator
   diff. The rule survives on the supersession/denominator hazard alone.
2. **§3.1's blocker** — real and correctly stated (verified refusals, one repo, 101
   imports). The multi-fixture change is required for B/D but is *less* contained than
   Step 1 implies: the "projects" half of it does not exist (C2), and the validator half
   collides with the Done-when's letter (I4).
3. **The 473** — reproduced exactly, from scratch and from the module. k = 1 is the right
   assumption and is structurally pinned, which the plan could state as a strength. The
   unsimulated pass is the right binding pass.
4. **The sizing** — bottom line reproduces (+342; C ≈ +1030; D ≈ +615), but two of three
   conversion figures in §2 are wrong (I1) and route D lands ~6 corroborations short in
   expectation at diversity's measured 97% conversion (I2).
5. **The density hazard** — real, not invented, but misframed (M2): it is the blast
   radius of a rule-1 failure, not a property of density itself, and rule 1's only leak
   is the extractor. If it fired undetected it would silently shrink the denominator;
   step 3's authored-vs-counted reconciliation plus the read-back's per-project
   superseded counts genuinely catch it — post-run, loudly, at the cost of a re-author.
6. **§6's disclosure** — adequate in substance and honestly worded; it does not soften
   T2.130's limit. Two additions: quote the bound with enough precision to show it is
   strictly under the bar (M4), and require k and the pass (unsimulated) to travel in the
   quoted line, which §7's last row already half-commits to.
7. **What the plan does not say** — the `default`-pinning of the diversity arm (C2, the
   largest omission), the conversion margin (I2), the wave rule, the controls guard, the
   authoring guard against control-claim collisions and replacement phrasing, the
   per-project cap value, and the live-call cost on the fixed model (M3, M5).

## Summary for the controller

The number is right, the blockers are real, and the route's economics reproduce — but the
plan cannot be executed as written. Its highest-stakes rule (§3.2's REPLACES ban) is
justified by a mechanism the code refutes while its real justification goes
under-credited; its recommended route's spread rationale rests on a repo-project knob
that does not exist, and the diversity arm is silently pinned to project `default` where
nothing would catch a spread-authored corpus except a short count at step 4; its Step 1
collides with the Done-when's "passes unmodified" without noticing; and its sizing table
misstates two conversions while route D as sized lands short of 473 in expectation.
Each fix is local — this is a rework of the plan document's claims and of Step 1's
design, not of the route, the target or the step skeleton.
