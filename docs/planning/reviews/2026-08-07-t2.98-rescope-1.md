**What actually decides the selective case:** at this scale the three candidate plans
price within ~5% of each other (serial Seq Scan ≈ heap pages + cpu·N; parallel Seq Scan
discounts only the CPU term and pays `parallel_setup_cost`; the full-index traversal is
pinned to the subject index's ~1,000 pages regardless of heap width). The worker count
steps 1→2 near 3,100 heap pages, which is why the width axis is *non-monotonic*
(125→traversal, 145→Seq Scan, 384→traversal). At 5%+ frequency the bitmap's heap access
approaches the whole heap, so the Seq Scan wins at every width tested — that half is
robust. And because heap pages grow with row width while the three-text-column index
does not, the traversal wins the selective case as the table gets wider or larger — the
knife-edge is an artifact of ~100–150-byte rows at ~120k scale, which is exactly where
all three prior measurements sat.

**Scoring the three accounts:**

1. **Original report** ("no scale would force a literal Seq Scan") — wrong, as both the
   review and the fix round showed and my sweep confirms at every narrow-row point.
2. **Review** ("the axis is value frequency, not row count") — both its data points
   reproduce at realistic fact widths, and its two-regime framing (selective →
   traversal, common → heap scan) is the correct account at realistic widths and the
   asymptotically correct one at scale. But its implied universal — selective probes get
   the traversal *at any table size* — fails on narrow-fact seeds, which is the fix
   round's genuine contribution.
3. **Fix round** — all three of its *measurements* are honest and reproduce: Seq Scan at
   every point of a narrow-fact sweep including 1 row in 4,000 (their cost 185.01, my
   157 on slightly narrower rows); the traversal returning under
   `max_parallel_workers_per_gather=0` at their width (their 5,240.72, my 5,113–5,308);
   the 5% probe going Seq Scan and the index fixing it (their 13.3→2.5 ms, my
   14.4→2.1 ms). Its *generalization* is falsified by the width axis — see N1.

## N1 · Important — the replacement conclusion overgeneralizes from a narrow-fact seed

**Where:** `docs/planning/reviews/2026-08-07-t2.98-report.md`, item 2 of the F1
correction ("Postgres's planner does not choose it *at all* at these scales once
statistics are real ... cheaper across the whole frequency range tested, not only at the
high end") and the "Corrected conclusion" paragraph ("a genuine heap Seq Scan ... at
every frequency and table scale measured, not only for a disproportionately common
value"); plus `docs/planning/reviews/2026-08-07-t2.98-fix-round-1-report.md:46-48`
("statistics freshness turned out to be the deciding factor, not frequency per se").

**What is wrong:** those claims are true only of the sweep's own seed, whose `fact`
column was ~20–35 characters. Real `knowledge_triples.fact` values are lifted sentences;
at fact ≈ 44 chars (avg row width 125 — still short for this table) and at 300 chars,
the planner chooses the full-index-traversal BitmapOr for a selective probe under
**wholly default settings and fresh statistics** — the plan the fix round says is never
chosen once statistics are real. The item-3 mechanism claim ("it is what Postgres falls
back to when it cannot spread the scan over parallel workers") fails in the other
direction: on my 20-char seed, disabling parallelism still yields a Seq Scan at every
frequency, so parallel workers are not the deciding factor either. Frequency remains a
real axis — at every width tested, 5% forced the heap scan and the traversal appeared
only at the selective end. Statistics freshness is a *precondition of measuring
anything*, not the crossover's cause.

**Why it matters:** same class as F1 — an overgeneralized checked conclusion recorded in
G-C's evidence trail, replacing one wrong universal with another. The G-C-load-bearing
disposition is unaffected (missing index = O(live set) work on *some* substrate, doc 08
§5.3's "degrades to a scan" right in substance, fix justified — all three accounts and
my sweep agree), which is why this is Important, not Critical.

**Fix:** one paragraph again. State the two-regime account with the knife-edge honestly:
a sufficiently common value forces a literal heap Seq Scan at every row width (measured);
for selective probes at ~120k scale the heap Seq Scan and the full-index traversal price
within a few percent and the winner flips with row width and parallel-worker
availability (measured both ways); wider rows or bigger tables push the selective case
to the traversal, since the heap grows and the index does not. Drop "not frequency per
se" and "does not choose it at all". The F2 docstring already states a compatible
account and needs no edit.
