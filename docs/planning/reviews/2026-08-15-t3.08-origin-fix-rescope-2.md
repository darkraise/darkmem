## New Finding B — Important: the one-slash fold regressed `file:` empty-authority spellings

Introduced by `e371a276`'s regex change (`auto_research.py:109`). The fold boundary
moved from {0 slashes}→`//` (with 1 slash incidentally becoming `///`) to
{0,1}→`//`. Every same-source pair straddling the one-vs-three-slash boundary
flipped from merge to split, verified differentially against the `f5f2b176` module:

| Pair (one source, two spellings) | Pre | Post | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| `file:/tmp/a.pdf` vs `file:///tmp/a.pdf` | **MERGE** (`/tmp/a.pdf` both) | **SPLIT** (`tmp/a.pdf` vs `/tmp/a.pdf`) | `str(httpx.URL("file:///tmp/a.pdf"))` → `'file:/tmp/a.pdf'` — the repo's own client emits the left spelling for the right; RFC 8089 §2 makes them one resource (empty authority) and WHATWG parses both with an empty host |
| `https:///example.test/a` vs `https:/example.test/a` | **MERGE** | **SPLIT** | `str(httpx.URL("https:///example.test/a"))` → `'https:/example.test/a'` — the raw href and its httpx spelling |
| `file:/tmp/a.pdf` vs `file://tmp/a.pdf` | split (correct: RFC 8089 says host `tmp` is a different resource) | **over-merge** | lesser, undercount direction |

The new fold makes `tmp` the host of `file:/tmp/a.pdf` — exactly the mistake the
regex's own comment refuses to make at three slashes. The rationale is therefore
inconsistent with itself: it protects `file:///`'s empty authority against a
three-slash fold while the one-slash fold destroys that same authority for the
spelling `httpx` actually emits. This is the same failure class as round 1's New
Finding A — a repair for "one page must not become two origins" creating exactly
that for a shape it did not consider — at the same instrument (`str(httpx.URL(...))`),
in the same direction (split = manufactured corroboration; two same-kind origins
≥ 7 days apart promote at N = 2), under the same mitigations (loop disarmed by
default, no config-row writer in the tree, promoted badges currently serve as
`candidate` while G-C stays open). Round 1 graded its instance Important under
those mitigations; consistency grades this one Important too. Not Critical:
operationally inert today, and it needs a `file:`-cited or triple-slash source
held under two spellings across runs.

**Repair:** make the fold scheme-aware — fold `:/{1,}` to `://` only for
authority-mandatory schemes (`http`, `https`), and normalise `file:/` to the
empty-authority form — or, minimally, exclude `file` from the one-slash fold.
Pin `file:/tmp/a.pdf` ≡ `file:///tmp/a.pdf` red-first, keep
`file://tmp/a.pdf` distinct, and pin `https:///…` against its httpx spelling.

### Judgement on the deliberate three-slash non-merge

Defensible only in part. For a scheme-agnostic fold, refusing three-plus slashes
is right — `file:///` is the canonical file spelling and folding it would mint a
false host. But no scheme-agnostic slash rule is correct for both `https:`
(browsers fold any slash count to `//`) and `file:` (one and three slashes mean
empty authority; two means a host), so the choice was always which pairs to leave
split — and the round's choice left both known straddling pairs split where the
old boundary left one, without saying so. The committed control is honest about
its reason; the reason does not survive the neighbouring case. A scheme-aware
fold dissolves the trade entirely, at which point the three-slash control should
assert the *https* pair merges and the *file* pair keeps its empty authority.

### The CHANGELOG sentence is falsified again, at the margin

`CHANGELOG.md:32-36`: *"the canonicalisation collapses the variation a fetcher
produces mechanically, so a refetch, a re-run or a paraphrase of one page adds no
origin."* The `file:` pair above **is** variation this repo's fetcher produces
mechanically, and it adds an origin. The sentence is far closer to true than
either predecessor and the new bullet (`:78-86`) is accurate — including "found by
the review of the fix, not by the fix's own tests", which this round repeats —
but the lead sentence again states a closure the code does not have. With New
Finding B repaired it becomes true; until then it should carry the docstring's
own phrasing (a tested property with named pins, not a guarantee).
