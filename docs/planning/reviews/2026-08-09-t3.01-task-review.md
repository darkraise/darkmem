## The decision under attack: stored rungs, not served

The premise — *nothing serves `verified_fact_count` today* — is **true and was
verified, not accepted**: `rg` over `backend/` and `frontend/` finds no importer
of `article_eligibility`, no route, no MCP tool, no job wiring; T3.02's runner
is still the `PlaceholderJobRunner`. The three-part argument in the module
docstring holds: doc 09 §4.1 states the floor in doc 08's stored labels
verbatim ("non-disputed corroborated/confirmed"); the output is a work list for
a consumer G-D holds shut; and articles' own labels are served through
`fact_badge`/`claim_gates` when T3.02 lands, so the reader-facing gate exists
downstream where the claim is actually made. A served floor here would ship the
scorer inert and gate one claim twice — I could not refute that.

**But the premise is enforced by nothing** (finding I-1). The registry entry in
`AGGREGATES_NAMING_THE_RUNG` is an honest *record* — the sweep pins the file
set, and only `pin_suggestions` gets a binding assertion
(`test_the_one_swept_aggregate_binds_the_rungs_the_gate_allows` asserts its
`raw_statuses_for_served` inversion; nothing analogous exists or can exist for
a doorless module). A future route serving `verified_fact_count` emits an int,
not a status: the label scan is blind to it, the aggregate sweep pins where the
SQL lives, not who calls it, and nothing reddens. The obligation fires only if
the door-builder happens to read the docstring or the sweep comment. The claim
"registered ... so that obligation is visible from the sweep" is accurate as
written — visible, not enforced — and the repo's standing pattern for
load-bearing premises is scan enforcement, not prose.

## Done-when evidence

**1. Eligibility is boolean and checked before scoring, proven by test — ✅,
with a caller-facing seam.** `_build` computes `eligible` (a real bool), and
the ineligible arm early-returns with `priority=None`
(`article_eligibility.py:395-401`); no path in the module computes a number for
an ineligible subject, and mutation drill A (score the ineligible arm) reddens
exactly `test_an_ineligible_subject_carries_no_priority_at_all`. I tried to
construct a scoring path and could not — but I **can** construct a selection
path (finding I-2): `order_for_budget` neither filters nor refuses
`eligible=False`, ranking a None-priority candidate at `0.0` via
`-(c.priority or 0.0)` (`article_eligibility.py:452`). Since
`candidate_subjects` deliberately returns ineligibles "for diagnosis", the
one-step misuse `order_for_budget(await candidate_subjects(...), config)` puts
ineligible subjects into the synthesis work list whenever the eligible pool is
smaller than the budget — reproduced with a driver. `select_for_synthesis`
filters correctly, so the shipped composition is safe; the guarantee is
conventional, not structural, and the docstring's "structural rather than
conventional" claim overstates it (the frozen dataclass also happily carries
`eligible=False, priority=7.0` — no `__post_init__`).

**2. Weights and budget are config rows with working validation — ✅, with one
hole.** One `config` row (`article_selection`) carrying all five parameters —
the repo's parameter-family idiom (`verification_ladder`, `job:*`), a fair
reading of the plural. Re-derived, not read: defaults with no row, floor
settable, budget bounding, unknown-key warning, unparseable-string fallback,
out-of-range clamp all reproduce; a weight is a real multiplier
(`w_central=0.0` drops the term, and my own driver confirmed the same for
`w_heat` — see M-1). The hole is `NaN` (finding I-3): a row value of `"nan"`
passes `float()`, defeats the `min(max(...))` clamp (NaN comparisons are
false), and for an int param crashes `get_synthesis_config` with
`ValueError: cannot convert float NaN to integer`
(`article_eligibility.py:214-215`) — reproduced. For a float param it returns
`w_*=nan` silently, poisoning every priority. Both outcomes violate the
function's own stated contract ("a bad row must not take the synthesis job
down ... must also never silently mean something other than what it says"),
on exactly the surface it names (operator-edited, no schema in front).

**3. One live article per (project, subject_key) enforced by constraint — ✅.**
Satisfied by unchanged code: `ux_knowledge_articles_live` in the baseline
(`0001_baseline.py:779-781`, partial unique on `(project, subject_key) WHERE
status <> 'superseded'`). The test asserts the **constraint**, not the
application — `IntegrityError` raised by the second `INSERT` statement itself,
before any flush — and the superseded-leaves-room test pins the partial
predicate. `_LIVE_ARTICLE_SUBJECTS_SQL`'s `status <> 'superseded'` matches the
index predicate verbatim, and matches `pin_suggestions`' live-article gate.
Sharpness gap: no test proves the pair-scoping (same subject_key, different
project must coexist) — an index on `subject_key` alone would pass the shipped
suite (M-2).

## The SQL, re-derived

- **Heat join cannot fan out**: `fact_access_stats.fact_id` is a PRIMARY KEY
  (`0001_baseline.py:695-700`), so the LEFT JOIN is 1:0..1 and `COUNT(*)`
  cannot be inflated by it. SUM-vs-MAX divergence from `review_ranking` is
  argued correctly (subject signal vs fact signal) and `review_ranking` really
  does use `MAX(mas.access_count)` (`review_ranking.py:400`).
- **The heat arm works but no shipped test exercises it** (M-1): nothing
  inserts a `fact_access_stats` row, so `read_heat` is 0.0 in every shipped
  test and a broken join column or a dropped SUM would stay green. My driver
  confirmed 10+5 lands as `read_heat=15.0` and that `w_heat` multiplies.
- **`MIN(e.entity_type)` / `MIN(subject_id)` grouping** (I-2b, folded into
  I-2's severity block below as its own finding — see Important 2b): grouping
  is by `name_lower` alone, and the two MINs are independent aggregates. With
  two entities sharing a name across types — legal by design, uniqueness is
  `(name, entity_type)` — I reproduced: entities `(redis, tool)` (2 facts) and
  `(redis, concept)` (1 fact) yield **one** candidate `redis::concept` with
  `verified_fact_count=3`, `eligible=True`, though neither typed subject alone
  clears the floor. The soft key takes the lexicographic MIN type (which the
  majority entity loses), and `entity_id` — the centrality probe — can come
  from the *other* entity than the one whose type named the key. A pin on
  `redis::tool` would not attach to that candidate and would mint a second,
  zero-fact eligible candidate for the same name. The name-only *count* is
  `pin_suggestions`' documented approximation (`knowledge_triples` carries no
  entity_type — soft ref, doc 08 §3), so pooling is convention-consistent; the
  arbitrary type-minting and type/id mispairing are this module's own, and
  unacknowledged. Production reachability is moderated by `fact_lift` grouping
  by name (one subject_id per name group per tick), but nothing constrains the
  rows: merges and re-lifts can produce the mixed state.
- **NULL / dangling `subject_id` is handled honestly**: MIN ignores NULLs so a
  resolved sibling supplies the id; an all-NULL group gets `entity_type
  'concept'` (the same fallback `normalize_entity_type` gives) and a NULL
  `entity_id`, whose centrality subquery compares against NULL and honestly
  yields 0. Reproduced with a mixed NULL/resolved group — one candidate, no
  crash, no phantom.
- **Project scoping is right**: facts filtered by project in the CTE; entities
  and relations are global by design (centrality is a graph property);
  `knowledge_articles` exclusion is project-scoped. asyncpg CAST discipline
  observed on every param, including `ANY(CAST(:statuses AS text[]))`.
- **Expired-only filter matches doc 09 §4.2's own gather predicate**
  (`expired_at IS NULL`, not `invalid_at`) and `pin_suggestions._FACT_COUNT` —
  consistent on both sides.

## Findings

**Important 1 — the doorless premise that licenses the stored-rung floor is
enforced by prose alone.** `test_claim_serving_gates.py:1945-1958`. The whole
stored-vs-served argument rests on "no route, no door", which is true today
but pinned by nothing: a future route serving `verified_fact_count` trips
neither the label scan (it's an int) nor the aggregate sweep (which pins where
SQL lives, not who calls it). The repo pins load-bearing premises with scans
(invariant 15, the RRF-constant scan, `verification_unmeasured`); this one is
cheap to pin the same way — assert no module under `app/api` (or outside the
synthesis job) imports `article_eligibility`, so the obligation the registry
entry records fires mechanically the moment it becomes due. Also note the
T3.02-adjacent leak path: "journals its inputs" + `GET /events` would put the
count on a served surface without any route ever importing this module.

**Important 2 — `order_for_budget` ranks and selects ineligible subjects.**
`article_eligibility.py:452`. `-(c.priority or 0.0)` silently adopts 0.0 for
the exact object the design says must never be ranked, and the function is the
published ordering seam (the tests call it directly). One plausible caller
mistake — ordering `candidate_subjects` output, which deliberately includes
ineligibles — fills spare budget slots with under-floored subjects; reproduced.
A one-line filter or refusal (`if c.eligible` / raise on `priority is None`)
makes the floor structural, which is what the module docstring already claims
it is.

**Important 2b — subject identity is minted from independent MINs over a
name-only group.** `article_eligibility.py:249-250`. Demonstrated: facts split
across same-named entities of different types pool into one candidate whose
floor crossing neither typed subject earned, keyed by the lexicographically
smallest type, with centrality read from a possibly different entity's id, and
with a pin on the majority entity's soft key failing to attach (minting a
second eligible candidate for the same name). At minimum the type/id pairing
should be consistent (take both from one entity — e.g. the mode or the fact-
majority entity) and the approximation acknowledged the way `pin_suggestions`
acknowledges its name-only count.

**Important 3 — `NaN` defeats the config clamp: crash for int params, silent
score poisoning for weights.** `article_eligibility.py:203-215`. Reproduced:
`{"synthesis_budget": "nan"}` logs "clamping to nan" (false) then raises
`ValueError` out of `get_synthesis_config` — the exact "take the synthesis job
down" the docstring forbids; `{"w_heat": "nan"}` returns NaN and poisons every
priority with no log at all. One `math.isfinite(parsed)` check in the fallback
branch closes both.

**Minor 1 — the heat arm has zero shipped coverage.** No test writes
`fact_access_stats`; a regression in the join or the SUM stays green. My
driver covered it this once; the suite should own it.

**Minor 2 — the constraint test does not prove pair-scoping.** A unique index
on `subject_key` alone would pass both shipped tests; one insert of the same
key under a different project pins `(project, subject_key)`.

**Minor 3 — dead code.** `_INT_PARAMS` (`article_eligibility.py:119`) and
`DEFAULT_SYNTHESIS_CONFIG` (`:141`) are referenced nowhere, including tests.

**Minor 4 — the priority formula deviates from doc 09 §4.1's stated linear
form.** The doc's stance is `w_facts·verified_count + w_heat·read_heat +
w_central·centrality`; the module applies `ln(1+x)` per signal first
(`article_eligibility.py:319-326`). The compression is real in `review_ranking`
(`ln(1.0 + ...)`, `review_ranking.py:492-497`) and the reasoning is sound, but
the docstring presents it as implementing the doc rather than as a recorded
deviation from the doc's literal formula; the damping changes what a measured
`w_*` tune means. Plan-mandated formula vs. shipped formula — controller may
consider it settled by the module's argument, but it should be settled on
purpose.

**Minor 5 — small inaccuracies in prose.** The `_PINNED_SIGNALS_SQL` comment
says pinned subjects' "heat and centrality still resolve ... through the
entity" — heat is hardcoded `0.0` in that arm (`article_eligibility.py:374`),
only centrality resolves (the 0 is *correct* — no verified facts means no
fact-keyed heat — but the comment claims a resolution that does not happen).
The pinned-key decode reimplements `pin_suggestions.decode_subject_soft_key`
inline (`:364`) instead of importing the function whose rsplit rationale is
already documented.
