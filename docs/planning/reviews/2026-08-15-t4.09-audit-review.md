## Findings

### Critical

**C1 — Missed assumption: `reembed_locks`, the re-embed doors' busy contract.**
`app/services/reembed.py:25-44` is a module-level in-process lock set
(`reembed_locks = ReembedLockSet()`), and it is the *only* guard behind
`POST /api/v1/reembed/{category}` and the reembed-all door
(`app/api/routes/reembed.py:141` `if not reembed_locks.try_acquire(category): raise
HTTPException(409, ...)`; `:244` for the all-categories loop). The audit never mentions the
file, the singleton, or the routes. What a second worker concretely causes, in the audit's
own vocabulary: the 409-busy contract goes false — a "run now" routed to worker B starts a
second whole-corpus re-embed of a category worker A is already running (the exact shape item
4 grades BREAKS); both loops select the same missing rows and pay the embed spend twice over
the *entire* missing set, which after an embedder rotation is the whole corpus; and both
broadcast `pipeline.reembed_batch.progress` for one category under two `batch_id`s, so the
UI progress oscillates. Stored vectors stay correct (same model, `ON CONFLICT` upserts,
`_assert_fingerprint` pins the partition), so the payload is item-22-class spend plus an
item-4-class false contract, not corruption. Graded Critical per this review's rubric — a
missed assumption is the one defect an enumeration audit exists to not have, and T4.10
cannot decide about a row that is not on the list — with the honest qualifier that the
mitigation class (in-process busy signal) is already represented by items 4 and 11, so the
*decision* the list feeds is unlikely to change. Fix: add the row (BREAKS by the audit's own
item-4 precedent, or BREAKS-narrow), and correct the 35/16 counts everywhere they appear,
including the amendment-log row.

### Important

**I1 — The classifier-resolution cache is named by the audit and absent from its inventory.**
The doc 02 §7 divergence list cites "the pipeline's 30-second classifier-resolution cache
(`pipeline.py:211`, `:234-239`)" as one of the caches doc 02's sentence misses — and the
35-row inventory has no row for it. It is real (`app/services/pipeline.py:210-211`,
`_resolve_classifier` at `:225-249`) and it is item 19's exact class with a 6× longer
window: a `classifier_kind` flip reaches other workers only at TTL expiry, so for up to 30
seconds two workers classify identical content through different classifiers. DEGRADES,
mild, self-healing — but the document is internally inconsistent about its own census: a
cache it demonstrably knows exists is excluded from the list its headline counts are
computed over. Fix: add a DEGRADES row referencing item 19.

### Minor

**M1 — Internal count contradiction: "twenty-eight" vs 35.** Finding 3 (analysis line 44)
says §11's option set "addresses one finding out of twenty-eight"; the inventory totals 35
(line 105) and the amendment-log row says "1 of 35". 16+11+8=35 is the correct arithmetic;
28 matches no partition of it (BREAKS+DEGRADES is 27) and reads as a stale draft residue. In
a decision input the headline denominators must agree or the wrong one gets quoted forward.

**M2 — Item 28's caveat under-describes what a breached margin does.** I verified the margin
is real: the flush deadline is 290 s (`buffer_flush.py:96-98`) measured from the tick start,
the claim happens at the top of the tick, and visibility is floored DB-side at 300 s from
`claimed_at` (`:124`), so a settle has ≥ 10 s plus the recover/sweep time — the audit's
account is correct. What it does not say: the settle statements carry no
`claimed_by`/`status='claimed'` guard — `_mark_done` (`:637-643`), `_release` (`:658-668`)
and `_fail_permanently` (`:697-705`) all update by bare `id` — so if the margin is ever
breached (a settle commit stalled > 10 s under DB contention), worker B's reclaim-and-reclaim
is silently overwritten by worker A's late settle: a row another worker is actively
processing gets stamped `done`, or an error masked, with no symptom. The ALREADY SAFE
verdict stands — the window needs a > 10 s stall in a handful of single-row UPDATEs — but
the caveat is the line T4.10 would read before ever considering shrinking the visibility
floor, and the blast radius of a breach belongs in it.

**M3 — Citation drift in the PPR paragraph.** The lead finding cites
`hybrid_search.py:472-473` for `if not ppr_weight: return []`; that guard is at `:469-470`
(`:472-473` is the second early return, the project check). Both precede any cache contact —
the `ppr_snapshot` import itself is deferred to `:474` — so the substance is untouched.
Every other citation I spot-checked (~30 across 20 files) was exact.

## Bottom line

The audit is sound where it reasons and thorough where it verifies — every verdict attacked
held, including both ALREADY SAFE calls the dispatch singled out, and its two sharpest
sentences (finding 2, and the reclaim docstring correction) are both true and both things
T4.10 genuinely needs. It fails its own bar in one place: "every" was the deliverable, and
`reembed_locks` — findable by the same `= [A-Z]\w+\(\)` singleton grep that finds
`retry_locks` — is not on the list, with a second, self-inflicted omission (the classifier
cache it names but never rows). Fix round: add the two rows, reconcile the counts (M1),
extend item 28's caveat (M2), fix one citation (M3), and update the amendment-log numbers.
No re-reasoning is required anywhere.
