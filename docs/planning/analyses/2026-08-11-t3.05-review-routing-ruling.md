# Ruling recommendation — doc 09 §4.4's "routes to review" clause

**Tier-3 analysis for the owner's ruling, 2026-08-12. Responds to
`2026-08-11-t3.05-held-resynthesis-review-routing.md`. Nothing is edited by this
document; it is the argument the owner rules on.**

**Recommendation in one sentence: route the *subject*, not the *draft* — a
non-withholding attention marker on the standing article's own columns,
T2.112's contradiction-suspected shape transplanted to the article axis — and
keep §4.4's sentence with its middle clause rewritten to say exactly that.**

---

## 1. The claimed impossibility is false as stated — and the correction dissolves the dichotomy

The escalation's load-bearing claim is: the review queue's only handle on an
article is `review_state`; `ux_knowledge_articles_live` admits one
non-superseded row per `(project, subject_key)`; therefore storing the failed
draft requires superseding the standing article, which the sentence forbids.

Both premises are true and the conclusion does not follow, because the index is
**partial**:

- `0001_baseline.py:785` — `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX ux_knowledge_articles_live ON
  knowledge_articles (project, subject_key) WHERE status <> 'superseded'`.
- `review_queue.build_articles_query` (review_queue.py:390) — the article
  lane's membership predicate is `review_state = 'open'` **with no status
  filter at all**.

So there exists a write the escalation says cannot exist: insert the failed
draft with `status='superseded'` at birth and `review_state='open'`. It does
not touch the standing article, it does not collide with the unique index, and
it appears in the review queue **today, with zero queue changes**. The
"impossibility" is not one.

That path is still wrong, and it matters *why*, because the real barrier is
what points at the right answer. Three things bar it, none of them the index:

1. **It poisons the status vocabulary and the lineage walk.** `'superseded'`
   means "was the live article, replaced by a successor" everywhere the corpus
   speaks (doc 04 §8.8's invariant-8 generalisation; `articles_channel.py`'s
   own comment; `articles_read._SUPERSEDES_SQL`). A born-superseded draft
   either fakes a pointer — `superseded_by = standing.id`, at which point
   `_SUPERSEDES_SQL` (`WHERE superseded_by = :id ORDER BY synthesized_at DESC
   LIMIT 1`) returns the rejected draft as the standing article's predecessor,
   *shadowing the genuine one*, and the reader walks lineage into prose that
   never served — or carries a NULL pointer, the exact anomaly the memory
   side's `chk_superseded_has_pointer` exists to forbid and that
   `resynthesise_subject`'s own docstring calls "silently losing the lineage".
2. **Invariant 14.** The draft's prose asserts claims its own measurement says
   the verified facts do not entail. Stored, it is durable in a vault with no
   delete path, servable by id on the detail door, forever. A surface may not
   assert a claim no evidence backs; neither should the vault durably hold one
   under an article status.
3. **Accumulation.** One junk row per held attempt — and (see §5) the shipped
   code attempts daily.

**The corrected statement is: the failed draft has no *honest* destination —
not no destination.** And once that is the statement, the sentence's "it"
unbinds from the draft. What §4.4 needs in front of a human is not the prose —
which the implementer is right that no reader should ever see — but the
**finding**: *this subject's current verified set cannot ground an article at
the bar*. That is a statement about the standing article's subject, and the
standing article's own row can carry it without touching `review_state`. The
escalation's dichotomy ("supersede a good article, or withhold a good
article") was an artifact of insisting the routed thing be the draft.

## 2. The corpus already solved this exact problem, on the fact axis

The held re-synthesis is the first article-side case where two things
`review_state` currently conflates come apart:

- **"needs human attention"** — true of the standing article's subject;
- **"not fit to serve as knowledge"** — false of the standing article, which
  remains the best grounded write-up of the subject in existence.

First synthesis conflates them legitimately: a new ungrounded article is both
unfit and attention-worthy, so `candidate` + `review_state='open'` (T3.02/T3.03,
`UNMEASURED_STATUS`/`UNMEASURED_REVIEW_STATE`) is exactly right, and doc 13
§5.2's withholding contract is doing its job. Re-synthesis is where they
diverge — which is precisely why the one column that encodes both at once
cannot carry it, and why every option that reaches for `review_state` (the
analysis's options 2-as-written and 3) fights the design instead of using it.

The fact axis hit this same divergence with the LLM contradiction scan and
settled it (T2.109 → T2.112, doc 08 §4.2 as amended): a
**contradiction-suspected fact keeps its rung, its badge and its place in
retrieval** while `contradiction_suspected_at` / `contradiction_counterpart_id`
— columns on the row, written by the authority, a cache the authority refreshes
every cycle, never a frozen verdict — put it in front of a human. The queue
derives the reason from the row's own columns (`_fact_reason`), the T2.62 rule
that `review_state` is pure queue membership with one derived writer is
untouched, and the alternative (the queue reading the finding from the
adjudicator) was tried and barred. Attention without withholding is a solved
shape in this corpus. The ruling below is that shape, applied once more.

## 3. The recommended ruling — option 4: the held marker

**A held re-synthesis writes an attention marker on the standing article's own
columns and journals the outcome; the review queue's article lane serves the
standing article under reason `resynthesis-held`; the article keeps its status,
its badge, its stale label and its place in retrieval; `review_state` is never
touched, so doc 13 §5.2's withholding contract cannot fire on a good article.
The failed draft is still discarded — prose whose own measurement says the
facts do not back it has no place in the vault under any status.**

### 3.1 Design-doc amendment (doc 09 §4.4 — replacement for the disputed sentence)

> A re-synthesis that fails the grounding gate (§4.3) does not supersede the
> standing article, and the failed draft is not stored — prose whose own
> measurement says the verified facts do not entail it has no place in the
> vault under any status. What routes to review is the **subject**: the job
> writes a held marker on the standing article's own columns
> (`resynthesis_held_at`, the measured coverage, the fact-set hash it was
> measured against) and journals a `resynthesis_held` event carrying the
> measurements, and the review queue's article lane serves the standing
> article under reason `resynthesis-held`. The marker is doc 08 §4.2's
> contradiction-suspected shape on the article axis — attention without
> withholding: the article keeps its status, its badge, its stale label and
> its place in retrieval, because the finding is about the subject's current
> verified set, not about the prose that is still the best grounded write-up
> of it. The marker is a cache the job refreshes: cleared in the same
> statement as the supersession that resolves it, and cleared by the tick when
> the article ceases to be stale, so it can never outlive the condition it
> names. `resynthesis_held_at` is when the hold opened, not when it was last
> confirmed; the coverage and hash refresh on every held attempt. So a subject
> never loses a good article to a bad re-synthesis — and a subject whose
> knowledge can no longer be grounded is a case a human sees, not a counter on
> a job tick.

Doc 13 §5.2 gains one sentence: a standing article carrying the held marker
**remains in the corroborated view**, rendering the marker beside the stale
label ("a re-synthesis was attempted and held at coverage X against bar Y"),
and the Review article lane lists it under `resynthesis-held`. Doc 04 §8.8's
column table gains the three columns and the partial index.

### 3.2 Schema (regenerate `0001_baseline.py` in place; serialises through the epic's schema owner)

- `knowledge_articles` adds:
  - `resynthesis_held_at TIMESTAMPTZ NULL` — when the hold opened
    (T2.112's `since` semantics: not refreshed while continuously held);
  - `resynthesis_held_coverage REAL NULL` — the last held attempt's measured
    claim-support coverage;
  - `resynthesis_held_hash TEXT NULL` — the fact-set hash that attempt was
    measured against (what lets a reviewer see whether the finding still
    describes the current set, and what the §3.5 retry policy keys on).
- `CREATE INDEX ix_knowledge_articles_resynthesis_held ON knowledge_articles
  (resynthesis_held_at) WHERE resynthesis_held_at IS NOT NULL` — the queue
  lane's own partial index, preserving the module's predicate-matches-index
  discipline rather than widening the `'open'` predicate (which the module
  docstring correctly says provably loses `ix_knowledge_articles_review`).

The baseline is not frozen; the cost is a regeneration and a dev-database
drop, and it must serialise through the epic's schema owner (note: another
lane is concurrently editing doc 12 — the baseline file is the shared mutable
file the CLAUDE.md warns about).

### 3.3 Code

- **`app/services/events.py`**: `RESYNTHESIS_HELD = "resynthesis_held"` joins
  `EVENT_TYPES`, taxonomy-first, landing with its emitter. Payload:
  `subject_key`, `coverage`, `threshold` (the §4.3 config bar, default 0.95),
  `claims`, `entailed`, `uncited`, `fact_set_hash` — **measurements, never
  prose** (the events journal's write-time-minimization rule). Object: the
  standing article (`object_type='article'`). This is honestly *about* the
  standing article — "an attempted replacement for this article was held" —
  and it is a distinct type rather than a `verified` event because the
  standing article's prose was not measured; a `verified` row here would
  poison any future article-side metric SQL over that stream.
- **`app/services/article_synthesis.py`**, `resynthesise_subject`, the
  `CoverageOutcome.HELD` branch: in the same transaction, `UPDATE` the
  standing article's three marker columns (`resynthesis_held_at =
  COALESCE(resynthesis_held_at, now())`; coverage and hash overwritten), then
  `record_event(RESYNTHESIS_HELD, ...)`, then return `(None, "held")` as
  today. Ordinary journal contract (log a `False`, never raise): the marker
  columns are the primary surface, the authority rewrites them on the next
  attempt, and no doc 17 denominator reads this stream — the strict
  `JournalWriteFailed` contract is reserved for rows whose loss silently
  shrinks a measurement.
- **Clear, two exits, both the authority's**: (a) `_SUPERSEDE_SQL` becomes one
  statement that sets `status='superseded'` *and* NULLs the three marker
  columns — same-statement discipline, exactly as the ladder writes
  `contradiction_suspected_at` in the same UPDATE as `verification_status`;
  (b) the re-synthesis tick, which already computes staleness for every live
  article, clears markers on marked articles that are no longer stale (the
  fact set reverted), so the marker cannot outlive its condition. Three exits
  total — grounded re-synthesis, fact set moved then grounded, fact set
  reverted — and all three clear without a human.
- **`app/services/review_queue.py`**: `build_articles_query` gains a second
  arm — a separate statement over `resynthesis_held_at IS NOT NULL AND
  review_state IS DISTINCT FROM 'open'`, matched to the new partial index
  (the `IS DISTINCT FROM` term prevents double-listing the corner case of an
  unmeasured-open standing article that also collects a held marker; such a
  row lists once, under the open arm, with the marker riding the projection).
  `_ARTICLE_COLUMNS` gains the three columns; `_article_reason` gains a
  `resynthesis_held_at IS NOT NULL` arm rendering `resynthesis-held`, ordered
  after `disputed` (graver condition wins, the `_fact_reason` rule).
  Invariant 15 holds by construction: the queue reads columns and an
  `events.py` constant; it never imports the dream module.
- **`app/services/articles_read.py` / schemas / frontend**: the three columns
  ride the list/detail projections so the article page can render the marker
  beside the stale label; the Review articles tab learns one reason string.

### 3.4 Tests that pin it

1. Held path writes the marker and the event, and `review_state` stays NULL,
   `status` unchanged — in one transaction.
2. **The non-withholding proof**: with the marker set, the retrieval channel
   and `list_articles` still return the article, badge intact — the article
   twin of `test_review_queue`'s a-queued-fact-stays-retrievable proof.
3. The queue's article lane lists the marked article with reason
   `resynthesis-held`; EXPLAIN pin that the arm's statement uses the new
   partial index (the module's existing discipline).
4. A successful re-synthesis leaves the superseded row with no marker
   (break-it proof per the runbook: split the clear out of `_SUPERSEDE_SQL`,
   watch the lane test fail on a superseded row appearing in the queue, then
   restore).
5. The tick clears the marker of an article that ceased to be stale.
6. `resynthesis_held_at` does not move across consecutive holds; coverage and
   hash do.

### 3.5 The retry policy the marker licenses (companion, separable)

An unreported cost of the shipped behaviour, verified in the code: **a
chronically held subject re-attempts every tick, forever, invisibly.** The
held path writes nothing, so the debounce anchor keeps matching the unchanged
fact-set hash, `staleness_settled` keeps returning True (the
matched-anchor path writes no new anchor), and every daily tick pays one
draft call plus one entailment call per claim, discards the result, and
increments an in-memory counter nobody persists. The only damper is
incidental: every ~90 days the anchor ages out of the lookback and the
subject waits one debounce window.

That loop was defensible only as the system's *sole* escalation path — keep
trying because nobody will ever look. Routing to review replaces it: with a
human in the loop, the tick can skip the attempt while
`resynthesis_held_hash` equals the subject's current verified-set hash
(counted under its own name, e.g. `held_suppressed`), converting an unbounded
daily spend into one attempt per fact-set change. If the owner values draft
nondeterminism (a borderline subject can clear on a re-roll), a config floor
(`resynthesis_held_retry_hours`) instead of full suppression. Either way the
spend becomes visible and bounded. This is separable from the routing ruling
but is listed here because the routing is what makes it safe.

### 3.6 What an operator sees that they do not see today

- A Review → Articles entry: *"`postgres` — re-synthesis held: claim-support
  coverage 0.42 against bar 0.95, measured 2026-08-12; fact set has (not)
  changed since"* — versus today's nothing.
- On the article page, beside the stale label: *"a re-fresh was attempted and
  held"* — versus today's bare stale label, which cannot distinguish
  "refresh pending" from "refresh attempted and failed", an invariant-14 gap
  the escalation itself names.
- A `resynthesis_held` event stream answering "how long has this subject been
  ungroundable, and at what coverage" — versus today's discarded measurement.
- (With §3.5) a `held_suppressed` counter where an invisible daily LLM spend
  used to be.

## 4. Costs and what is given up

**Costs.** A baseline regeneration and dev-database drop, serialised through
the epic's schema owner; three columns and one partial index for one opener
(the fact axis pays two columns for the same shape — precedented); a second
query arm in the article lane; one reason string and one marker rendering for
the frontend to learn; one event type.

**Given up, deliberately.** The failed draft's prose survives nowhere — a
reviewer sees the measurement, never the text, so "diff the held draft against
the standing article" is not a thing this ruling enables. If that need is
ever demonstrated, the sanctioned home is a bounded excerpt in the
`resynthesis_held` payload under the journal's copy-only-when-needed rule —
never an article row. Also given up: the implementer's argument that *nothing*
should be recorded because the measurement was "of prose no reader will ever
see". That argument proves too much — the coverage number is evidence about
the *subject's verified set's* synthesisability, it was measured and paid for,
and a measured claim beats an argued one; discarding it is the one thing this
corpus consistently refuses to do with numbers.

## 5. What would have to be true for the rejected options to be right

- **Option 1 (accept; strike the clause).** Right if held outcomes prove
  dominated by judge noise — transient holds that would spam the queue — or if
  the owner rules that failure-to-refresh is operational telemetry outside the
  truth-review remit. Note that striking the clause also silently ratifies the
  §3.5 retry loop as the permanent escalation path, and permanently discards
  the only number the system produces about a subject's groundability. If
  option 4's queue entries turn out noisy in practice, the marker's hash
  already gives the damping knob (only route after N holds on the same hash)
  without re-opening this ruling.
- **Option 2 (queue reads the journalled held event).** Right if the schema
  change were unavailable — owner refusal, or post-freeze contention — since
  membership is derivable with no writer and no schema: live articles with a
  `resynthesis_held` event since their own `synthesized_at` (the
  `synthesized_at` bound keeps the partitioned-events scan honest, the undo
  precedent). Rejected because the corpus ran this exact experiment on the
  fact axis and the column won (T2.112): the queue's reason must derive from
  the row's own columns, and a temporal join on every queue page load is the
  cost the status-plus-partial-index design was chosen to avoid. (The
  invariant-15 argument that decided T2.112 does not bind here — the event
  constant lives in `events.py`, not the dream module — so this option is
  merely worse, not forbidden.)
- **Option 3 (a non-`open` `review_state` value).** Right only if T2.62 were
  overturned. It re-makes `review_state` a multi-value state machine whose
  values carry serving semantics — the exact several-writers-over-one-column
  ambiguity T2.62 removed — and it still needs a second partial index (the
  existing one indexes only the literal `'open'`), so it saves nothing over
  the marker while spending settled semantics. The analysis's framing of it
  as "a schema change to a frozen-in-practice column" actually *understates*
  the objection: the column is bare TEXT with no CHECK, so the value itself is
  free — what it costs is the design.
- **Born-superseded draft storage (the option the impossibility claim hid).**
  Right never, on current evidence: it works mechanically today (§1) but fakes
  a lifecycle, and either corrupts the `_SUPERSEDES_SQL` lineage walk or
  violates the superseded-has-pointer convention, while durably storing
  ungrounded prose. Its one virtue — preserving the draft — is served
  strictly better by an event-payload excerpt if ever needed.

## 6. Relation to T3.05 as shipped

The shipped conservative half is correct and survives intact: discard the
draft, leave the standing article untouched, count `held` as its own outcome.
This ruling adds the routing on top; nothing lands in reverse. The escalation
itself dissolves in the sense the brief asked about: the claimed impossibility
is disproved (§1), the sentence is satisfiable as amended (§3.1), and the
change set is an ordinary follow-up task — schema regen through the epic's
schema owner, service + queue + tests as specified — not a rewrite of
anything T3.05 delivered.
