# Tier-3 escalation — doc 09 §4.4's "routes to review" has nowhere to route

**Raised 2026-08-11 while executing T3.05. Owner ruling needed.**
T3.05 is **not blocked on it**: all three done-when clauses are delivered and
tested. What is open is one sentence of §4.4 that the implementation cannot
satisfy as written, and the conservative half of which it does satisfy.

## The sentence

> A re-synthesis that fails the grounding gate (§4.3) does not supersede the
> standing article — it routes to review, so a subject never loses a good
> article to a bad re-synthesis.

The first clause and the purpose clause are implemented: a draft measured below
the coverage threshold does **not** supersede, and the standing article is left
exactly as it was, still labelled stale. The middle clause — *it routes to
review* — has no available destination.

## Why it has no destination

The review queue's handle on an article is `knowledge_articles.review_state`, a
column on an article **row**. Routing the failed draft to review therefore
requires storing it, and doc 04 §8.8's `ux_knowledge_articles_live` admits one
non-superseded row per `(project, subject_key)`. So storing the failed draft
means superseding the standing article first — which is precisely what the
sentence's own first clause forbids.

The other reading — open review on the *standing* article — is worse, and it
fails for a reason the corpus states elsewhere: doc 13 §5.2 makes an article
with `review_state='open'` "withheld from the corroborated view and routed to
Review, not shipped as knowledge". That withholds a good article because a
*different* draft could not be grounded, which is the harm the sentence exists
to prevent, arrived at by obeying it.

## What was implemented instead

The draft is discarded, the standing article stands unchanged, and the tick
counts the outcome as `held` — its own count, deliberately not folded into
`failed`, so a corpus of thin subjects does not read as a vendor outage. No
event row is written against the standing article, because nothing about *it*
transitioned; writing one would put a measurement in the journal that was taken
of prose no reader will ever see.

**The cost, stated plainly:** a subject whose re-synthesis repeatedly fails
grounding is visible only as a counter on a job tick and a stale label on the
article. No human is prompted. If the corpus's knowledge genuinely moved and
the article can no longer be re-grounded, that is a state a reviewer should
probably see.

## What a ruling would decide

1. **Accept it** — the stale label plus the `held` count is the whole surface,
   and §4.4 loses "it routes to review". Cheapest, and honest, since the label
   already tells a reader the article predates the change.
2. **A fifth review opener**, on the article axis rather than the fact axis:
   the queue reads a journalled held re-synthesis rather than a column, the way
   `REVIEW_OPENERS` appends a predicate rather than a writer. This is the
   shape the corpus already uses for openers and it needs no second live row —
   but it puts an article-side opener in a registry that is fact-side today,
   and that is a design call, not an implementation detail.
3. **Something else** — e.g. a `review_state` value that is not `open` and
   therefore does not withhold. That is a schema change to a frozen-in-practice
   baseline column and a new serving state for the frontend to learn.

Reading 2 is the one that matches the corpus's own habits, which is why it is
not being taken in the loop: it widens the review queue's remit, and T2.112
built that surface for facts.
