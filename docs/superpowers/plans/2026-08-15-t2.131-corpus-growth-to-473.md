# T2.131 — step-level plan: grow the promotion corpus to a settling window

**Status:** plan, 2026-08-15. **Awaiting Fable-tier review; nothing here may be
executed before that review** (runbook §3 — Size L). Written after the owner
directed the lane down the independent probe's corpus-growth route rather than
T2.130's bound-rule switch.

**What this plan is not:** it does not switch the audit's scored rule, move the
0.01 bar or the 0.95 confidence, relax any corpus validator, or author a single
capture. It sizes the work, names two blockers the task text could not have
known about, and puts the routes in front of the reviewer with their costs.

## 1. The target, and why it is not the number that was suggested

Derived with the committed instrument (`promotion_metrics.corroborations_required`,
`clopper_pearson_upper`), at the corpus's actual **k = 1**:

| n | point estimate | 95% upper bound | clears the bound? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 131 (today, unsimulated) | 0.0076 | 0.0357 | no |
| 300 (as suggested) | 0.0033 | 0.0157 | no |
| **473 (the target)** | 0.0021 | **0.0100** | **yes** |

299 is the **k = 0** window; the suggestion carried it onto a k = 1 corpus. The
gate ledger's "corpus-growth route" section holds the full table and the
structural point underneath it: the corpus has k = 1 *because* T2.129 armed it,
and the arming is the only reason the audit is not vacuous, so 299 is only ever
the price of the meaningless version.

**Target: ≥ 473 in-window corroborations on the unsimulated pass, with k still
exactly 1.** The unsimulated pass is the binding one — under the simulated clock
the number is inflated by a bucket that does not survive an honest clock.

## 2. What converts, measured on the committed corpus

246 memories yield **131** unsimulated corroborations (buckets: diversity 98,
none 35) and 149 simulated. The three promoting groups convert very differently:

| Group | Design | Memories per corroboration | Counts unsimulated? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `diversity` | 1 memory origin; the repo fixture's parsed import supplies the second origin *and* the second kind (N = 2) | **≈ 1**, plus one fixture import | yes (98) |
| `none` | 3 sessions inside one week, no gate fires, default N = 3 met exactly | **≈ 2.7** | yes (35) |
| `time_gap` | 2 sessions ≥ 7 days apart | ≈ 1.8 | **no — 0** |

**`time_gap` must not be grown.** All 18 of its simulated promotions exist only
under the simulated capture clock (T2.129's unsimulated pass); every capture
added there is spent on a bucket the target cannot count.

**+342 corroborations are needed.**

## 3. Two blockers the task text could not have known about

### 3.1 The diversity arm cannot scale inside one repo

`validate_diversity_group_against_fixture` refuses **two claims naming the same
dependency** — deliberately, because they would converge on one fact and the
bucket would carry fewer claims than the corpus declares. So every diversity
claim needs its **own distinct dependency**, and `repo_fixture.json` holds
exactly **one** repo (101 imports today, one per claim).

Growing diversity by +342 therefore means a single stand-in service repo with
**443 distinct third-party dependencies**. That is not a repository a working
agent could plausibly have indexed, and plausibility is not decoration here —
it is the premise the whole corpus rests on (`_notes`: *every entry is a capture
that a working agent could plausibly have made*). The arm hits a plausibility
ceiling, not an effort ceiling.

**Consequence:** either the fixture becomes multi-repo — `load_repo_fixture`,
`REPO_FIXTURE_PATH` and `prepare`'s wiring all assume one — or the growth comes
mostly from `none` at ~2.7 memories each.

### 3.2 Scale raises the chance of accidental supersession, which shrinks the denominator

`detect_supersessions` fires within a project when a new memory carries a
REPLACES-family relation whose targets overlap an active memory's
`technologies`, inside the 90-day window. Pass 1 of T2.129 failed exactly this
way: three same-claim captures retired each other and `supersession_hold`
withheld the promotion. That risk is per-project density, and this task
multiplies density by ~2.4.

**Two rules follow, and both are load-bearing:**

- **No new capture may carry a REPLACES-family relation** (`REPLACES`,
  `IMPROVES_ON`, `BUILDS_ON`). One that does can arm `inverse_replacement`, and
  a second dispute moves the target from 473 to **628** — the growth would
  outrun its own goal.
- **Density is capped per project.** A supersession that fires silently removes
  a corroboration from the denominator, so the read-back must reconcile
  authored claims against counted corroborations and fail loudly on a gap.

## 4. The routes, with costs

| Route | New captures | Fixture work | Code change | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — diversity only, one repo | ≈ +342 | +342 imports (443 total) | none | **Rejected.** §3.1: implausible repo |
| B — multi-repo diversity | ≈ +342 | 3–4 fixtures, ~110 deps each | **yes** — multi-fixture loader | Viable; spreads projects, which also answers the monoculture concern |
| C — `none`-heavy | ≈ +1030 | none | none | Viable but ~5.2× the corpus, and concentrates in one project unless spread |
| D — mixed, ~60/40 B/C | ≈ +205 diversity + ~410 `none` = **+615** | 2–3 fixtures | yes | **Recommended** |

**Why D.** It buys the plausibility that A cannot and the spread that C cannot,
at a capture count between them. The reviewer should push back on the split —
it is a judgement, not a derivation, and the honest ratio depends on how many
plausible service repos the fixture can carry before the fixtures themselves
look manufactured.

## 5. Steps

**Step 0 — Fable review of this plan.** Attack §3's two blockers, the §4 route
choice, and §6's disclosure. Nothing below starts first.

**Step 1 — Multi-fixture support (route B/D only).** Widen the fixture loader to
carry several repos with their own ids and projects; the diversity validator
becomes per-repo. Tests first, including the refusals in §3.1 which must keep
biting per repo rather than globally.

**Step 2 — Author the growth, in batches, spread by project.** Each batch:
plausible captures, distinct dependencies, no REPLACES-family relation, then
every validator (`validate_corroboration_design`,
`validate_claim_key_convention`, `validate_diversity_group_against_fixture`,
`validate_arming_design`, `validate_blocked_hold_design`) green before the next.
Commit per batch — the corpus is one large mutable file and a lost batch is
expensive.

**Step 3 — Density reconciliation.** Assert authored claims == counted
corroborations per project, and 0 unexpected superseded rows outside the
blocked-hold group. §3.2's silent failure is the one this catches.

**Step 4 — Both evidence passes.** Unsimulated first, because it carries the
target. Report the bound, the point estimate and the at-the-bar pass
probability under both rules, with rule 2's latency/token twin — absent from
four consecutive runs before `1713004e` and not to be dropped again.

**Step 5 — Independent verdict.** A party that did not author the growth
reproduces from committed state and records the verdict, as T2.129's done-when
required and got.

**Step 6 — Ledger and progress**, with §6's disclosure attached to the number.

## 6. The disclosure that travels with the result

Stated here so it is not composed after the number is known.

Reaching 473 makes the audit **decisive about a corpus its own measurer wrote**.
The numerator is one authored dispute; the denominator will be ~473 authored
corroborations. T2.130 already recorded the limit — *a well-powered rule over
authored evidence is a better instrument measuring the same thing* — and this
task does not lift it, it sharpens the instrument. A future reader meeting
"473 corroborations, bound 0.0100, CLEARS" must meet, in the same breath, that
every one of the 473 was written to be counted.

**This is not an argument against the instruction.** The owner chose the route
and the route is sound on its own terms: a settled bound is strictly better than
an unsettled one, and the alternative — switching the scored rule — remains
open and untaken. It is the caveat that has to survive the trip from here to
wherever the number gets quoted.

## 7. Where this goes wrong quietly

| Failure | Catch |
|---|---|
| A new capture carries a REPLACES-family relation, k becomes 2, the 473 target is silently wrong | Step 2 validator gate; step 3 asserts k == 1 before the run |
| Density causes a supersession, a corroboration vanishes, the count is short with no error | Step 3's authored-vs-counted reconciliation |
| Growth lands in `time_gap` and the unsimulated number does not move | §2's table; step 4 runs unsimulated first |
| A unique `origin_key` per memory reaches 473 instantly while being one assertion relabelled | `validate_corroboration_design` unchanged; the diff must show no validator weakened |
| The fixtures themselves start looking manufactured | Step 0 sets the plausible-repo ceiling before authoring, not after |
| 473 is quoted without its `k` | The ledger section and §1 both state it; the report prints k beside n |
