**What else v2 got wrong:** it spent its margin making 490 the floor and then
sized to land at 489.5 in expectation — v1's "sized exactly, misses its own

introduced were four-decimal values with a fabricated fifth digit. Every figure

**What survived both rounds unchanged:** 473 as the k = 1 derived minimum, §3.1's
plausibility blocker, §3.3's project confinement, and the §3.2 withdrawal.

**What this plan is not:** it does not switch the audit's scored rule (T2.132 did,

## 0. The two Step-0 adjudications — DECIDED

Both were ruled by the Fable re-review as escalation target (autonomous-loop §4);
neither was ruled owner-only, and the owner may override either.

**(a) Plausible-repo ceiling: six repos total** — the existing `payments-gateway`
plus at most five new, each new one carrying **40–90** distinct third-party
imports and none exceeding the existing repo's 101. Recommended shape: **4–5 new
repos averaging ~65–75 imports**, yielding ~260–340 claimable (repo, dependency)
pairs. Three properties, because manufactured-ness is a shape and not a count:
sibling repos **must share** a common core (a fleet with pairwise-disjoint
dependency sets is itself the manufactured signature); each new repo keeps
**≥ ~10% of its imports unclaimed** (the existing fixture claims 101 of 101 — a
claim manifest wearing a repo's clothes); and each repo's dependencies must be
**role-coherent** with its stated service, inside `corpus.json`'s existing world.
**If a split needs more pairs than the ceiling yields, the split moves toward
`none`; the ceiling does not move.**

**(b) The Done-when validator conflict: INTENT, granted with conditions.** The
per-repo form of `validate_diversity_group_against_fixture` preserves the
invariant's substance. The invariant the letter protects is claim-to-fact
bijectivity, and under per-repo subjects it survives structurally: `triple_key`
embeds the subject, the code lift's candidate match joins on canonicalised
subject name within `CODE_FACT_PROJECT`, and each code origin is per-repo
(`source_ref = rp.id`) — so cross-repo seconding has **no code path**. The ruling
rests on code the reviewer read independently, having been told in the brief that
this plan's author wrote both the clause and the plan needing it widened.

The letter reading's cost, stated so the choice is visible: the existing repo is
claim-saturated (101 claims ↔ 101 imports), so letter closes the diversity arm
entirely and forces an all-`none` route at ≈ +1092 captures (5.4×) — 1.9× the
cost, buying no independence, since both arms are authored by the same party
either way.

**Five conditions, all required.** v2's two are necessary and not sufficient:

1. Each claim binds to **exactly one** fixture repo.
2. Cross-repo dependency ambiguity is refused as dependency ambiguity is today.
3. **The widening-delta pair test**: one test asserting side by side that two
   claims naming one dependency in *different* repos are admitted **and** two in
   the *same* repo are still refused. This is what makes "no validator weakened
   beyond the adjudicated widening" a property of the diff rather than prose.
4. **Subject binding enforced, not assumed**: zero named repos is refused (not
   only ambiguity), the named repo must itself import the claimed dependency, and
   the loader refuses duplicate repo names/ids across fixtures. Subject binding is
   the entire mechanism making the facts distinct; a claim leaning on dependency
   uniqueness alone leaves the extraction's subject unpinned and re-opens the
   silent one-origin hold.
5. **Per-repo reconciliation at Step 3.**

and carry other groups' facts, so two of its three figures were wrong. Recounted

|---|---|---|---|

| `time_gap` | 2 sessions ≥ 7 days apart | 2.00 (36 → 18) | **no — 0** |

**Claims are not corroborations, and v2's blended constant hid where the gap
lives.** The committed census

| Source | Corroborations | Claims | Conversion |
|---|---|---|---|

So the shortfall is **3, all in `diversity`**, not "2, unexplained". The
mechanism is visible in the same file: the code lift creates 4 born facts over
102 sources — the 1 language edge plus the code halves of the 3 claims whose
memory-side extraction never converged (98 + 4 = 102). And the 131st
corroboration is the armed subject's, which growth does not reproduce.

**Sizing therefore uses per-arm conversion and no blend**: 0.9703 for
`diversity`, 1.0000 for `none`, and the armed subject stays in the 131 as a
constant. v2's 0.985 flattered the arm doing most of the growing and
mis-calibrated Step 3's reconciliation in both directions.

**The 3 unconverged claims were identified by SQL before the 0.9703 was trusted
across ~264 new names, and the cause is systematic** (executed 2026-08-15
against the preserved unsimulated database `darkmem_bench_t2129nosim`; the
optional item C-v2-1 left open and the delta check recorded as owed). Four facts
in `default` carry a `code`-kind provenance row and no `memory`-kind one — the
`payments-gateway USES python` language edge plus **`elasticsearch`, `redis` and
`requests`** — and the memory halves are all present under a *different* key:

| Claim | memory side lifted | code side asserted | why they did not meet |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pg-elasticsearch` | `payments-gateway DEPENDS_ON elasticsearch` | `payments-gateway USES elasticsearch` | rel_type |
| `pg-redis` | `payments-gateway DEPENDS_ON redis` | `payments-gateway USES redis` | rel_type |
| `pg-requests` | **`acquirer client` DEPENDS_ON requests** | `payments-gateway DEPENDS_ON requests` | subject |

Two of the three are one deterministic rule: `code_deriver._relation_type_for`
asserts `USES` when the dependency's entity type is a `database`, `service`,
`protocol` or `language`, and `DEPENDS_ON` otherwise, while a memory reading "X
depends on Y" always lifts `DEPENDS_ON`. Two rel_types are two `triple_key`s.
Screening the committed 101 imports through that same function returns
**exactly `elasticsearch` and `redis` and nothing else** — the shortfall is not
a sample of a rate, it is the whole population of a decidable predicate. The
third is an authoring drift: `pg-requests` names a second component in a
trailing clause ("inside the synchronous acquirer client") and the extractor
promoted that to subject.

**So the honest prior the delta check asked about is answered, and it points the
other way from the one it feared.** The systematic cause is *screenable offline*
and is now refused at authoring time by a test, and the drift cause is avoided
by keeping the repo the grammatical subject. New claims should therefore convert
**at or above** 0.9703, not below it — which is why the sizing keeps 0.9703
unchanged rather than banking the improvement. **The three existing claims are
deliberately not re-worded**: the disclosable +3 is real, but re-wording them
moves the very constant Step 3 reconciles against, and `redis` and
`elasticsearch` cannot be verified fixed without spending a live run.

**`time_gap` must not be grown.** All 18 of its promotions exist only under the
simulated capture clock, and the target rides the unsimulated pass.

**To reach an expected 500 from 131: +369 corroborations → 377 claims** at the
recommended split below.

## 3. The two blockers

### 3.1 The diversity arm cannot scale inside one repo — unchanged from v1, confirmed by review

`validate_diversity_group_against_fixture` refuses **two claims naming the same
dependency**, deliberately, since they would converge on one fact.
`repo_fixture.json` carries exactly **one** repo (101 imports, one per claim).
Growing diversity inside it means one stand-in service repo carrying **365
distinct third-party dependencies** at the recommended split, or **482** at an
all-diversity route — not a repository anyone indexed, and plausibility is the
premise the corpus rests on (`corpus.json`'s `_notes`).

The ceiling is plausibility, not effort. Escaping it needs a **multi-repo
fixture**: `load_repo_fixture`, `REPO_FIXTURE_PATH` and `prepare`'s wiring all
assume one. §0(a) now fixes that fleet at six repos total; §0(b) makes the
per-repo validator admissible.

**Note the existing repo is claim-saturated** — 101 claims against 101 imports,
bijective under the committed validator — so it contributes no further growth.
Every new diversity claim comes from a new repo.

### 3.2 What actually threatens k = 1 — v1's mechanism was refuted

**v1 claimed a new capture carrying a REPLACES-family relation would arm a second
dispute and move the target to 628. The code refutes it, and the correction is
recorded rather than quietly swapped.**

- `inverse_replacement` arms on a **live inverse fact** in the same project —
  `subject_name`/`object_name` transposed, `rel_type` in the family,
  `expired_at IS NULL`, windows overlapping, a non-echo witness. A lone stray
  REPLACES edge satisfies none of that.
- **k = 2 is unreachable in any corpus the committed loader admits.** The loader
  refuses `wave != 1` outside the arming groups, so an accidental inverse pair
  lands whole in wave 1. There the veto is checked ahead of the count, the fact
  is disputed straight out of `candidate`, and it never emits the `corroborated`
  event the audit's numerator joins on
  (`_FALSE_PROMOTION_AUDIT_SQL` requires corroborated-then-disputed on a
  `(created_at, id)` tuple compare). It lands in neither numerator nor
  denominator.

**So the rule "no new capture carries a REPLACES-family relation" is kept, but
demoted from a k-guard to hygiene**, and the 473 → 628 scenario is withdrawn:
reaching it would require weakening a validator, which the constraints forbid.

**What does threaten the count is supersession, and it is a different function
than v1 said.** v1 framed it as a function of density; it is a function of
*REPLACES-emitting captures × same-project in-window candidates*. Under the
hygiene rule the base rate is structurally zero and density multiplies only the
blast radius of a rule failure. Two consequences an executor needs: a
supersession that fires silently removes a corroboration from the denominator
with no error, and **the exposure decays with the calendar** — the existing
June–July absolute dates begin leaving the 90-day window around **2026-08-31**,
so Step 3's reconciliation bites hardest when run promptly after authoring.

### 3.3 The blocker v1 missed entirely: diversity is locked to one project

`fact_lift.CODE_FACT_PROJECT` is the constant `"default"`, and `repositories`
carries no project column. Code-derived corroboration therefore only ever matches
facts in `default` — which is why all 101 existing diversity memories sit there.
Its own docstring states the consequence: a memory captured under a *different*
project asserting the same triple lifts to a **different** fact and the two do
not corroborate.

**v1's route B/D premise — that multi-repo diversity spreads projects — is
false.** More repos buy more distinct dependencies, which is what §3.1 needs;
they buy no spread at all. A spread-authored diversity claim would silently hold
at one origin per claim, refused by no validator (none reads project) and visible
only as a short count at Step 4.

**So the monoculture concern lands in the arm v1 thought relieved it**, and the
only lever for project spread is `none`, at 3× the memories.

## 4. The route, sized on per-arm conversion to an expected 500

Diversity costs 1 capture + 1 (repo, dependency) pair per claim and converts at
0.9703; `none` costs 3 captures per claim and converts at 1.0000. Sized so the
**expectation** clears 490 with headroom rather than landing on it:

| Split (diversity share of claims) | Claims | Diversity claims / captures | `none` claims / captures | Total captures | Corpus | E[corroborations] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% | 381 | 381 / 381 | 0 / 0 | **381** | 627 (2.5×) | 500.7 |
| **70%** | **377** | **264 / 264** | **113 / 339** | **603** | **849 (3.5×)** | **500.2** |
| 50% | 375 | 188 / 188 | 187 / 561 | 749 | 995 (4.0×) | 500.4 |
| 0% | 369 | 0 / 0 | 369 / 1107 | 1107 | 1353 (5.5×) | 500.0 |

**Recommended: 70% — 264 diversity claims and 113 `none` claims, 603 captures.**
264 diversity claims sit inside §0(a)'s ceiling (~260–340 claimable pairs at 4–5
new repos) without strain. 100% is cheapest and leaves `default` holding 482 of
514 promoting claims with no spread anywhere. 0% triples the authoring to buy a
spread across projects that are still authored by the same party.

The split is a judgement; the ceiling that bounds it is now decided (§0a), and
the two move together — **if the diversity share ever needs more pairs than the
ceiling yields, the split moves toward `none`, never the ceiling.**

**Diversity captures land in `default` and that is not negotiable** (§3.3). The
disclosure in §6 says so rather than implying a spread the corpus does not have.

## 5. Steps

**Step 0 — DONE.** Both adjudications are decided (§0). What remains before
authoring is a delta check that v3 contains exactly the re-review's corrections
and nothing else.

**Step 1 — Multi-fixture support, under §0(b)'s five conditions.** Widen the
loader to carry several repos with their own ids; the diversity validator becomes
per-repo. All five conditions are requirements, not aspirations, and conditions
3 and 4 are the ones that make the grant honest rather than asserted: the
**widening-delta pair test** (same dependency, different repos → admitted; same
dependency, same repo → still refused, asserted side by side), and **enforced
subject binding** (zero named repos refused, the named repo must import the
claimed dependency, duplicate repo names/ids across fixtures refused), with the
same word-boundary rigor as the existing dependency match. Tests first.

**Step 2 — Author the growth, in batches.** Each capture: plausible content, wave
1 (the loader enforces it; stating it as design is also what pins k), no
REPLACES-family relation. Diversity claims name a distinct dependency and their
subject repo, in `default`. `none` claims are three same-project captures with
distinct origins spanning **under 7 days**. Every validator green before the next
batch; commit per batch, since the corpus is one large mutable file.

**Growth content must not re-assert a control claim's triple.** `claim_key`
groups by key, not content, so a differently-keyed capture asserting the `echo`
claim's content in `ledger` would add an origin and **promote a designed
refusal** — and no validator refuses it. Batch review checks content, not just
keys.

**Step 3 — Reconciliation, per arm and per repo — not against a blended
constant.** `none` claims reconcile at **1.000** (32/32 today; a miss there is a
defect, not variance). Diversity claims reconcile at **0.9703**, and
**additionally per repo** under §0(b) condition 5, since all of them live in
`default` and a project-level check cannot see which repo's claims fell short.
Also: 0 unexpected superseded rows outside the blocked-hold group; k still
exactly 1; the echo and under-sourced controls still at `candidate`
(`_CONTROL_FACTS`, `CONTROL_OBJECT_NAMES`). Run promptly after authoring, per
§3.2's calendar decay.

**The top-up protocol, written before it is relied on** (C-v2-1's fix prose
asked for it; it did not reach v3's text and the delta check recorded it as
owed). **A Step-3 shortfall is repaired by authoring extra `none` claims, three
captures each, until the expected count clears 490 again.** It is the mechanical
repair: `none` converts at 1.000, needs no fixture edge, touches no validator
and cannot reach §0(a)'s ceiling, so a top-up is bounded authoring rather than a
re-opened adjudication. It is *not* repaired by adding diversity claims — those
need new (repo, dependency) pairs and press on the ceiling — nor by re-sizing
the target. The +10 corroborations of headroom exist so this is unlikely to be
needed; §0(a)'s split rule covers the ceiling case, and this covers the
conversion-shortfall case, which is the other one.

**Step 4 — Both evidence passes.** Unsimulated first — it carries the target.
Report the bound to five decimals, the point estimate, and the at-the-bar pass
probability under both rules, with rule 2's latency/token twin. **Budget the
first run as live**: ~+603 uncached extraction calls against the gate-fixed
`deepseek-v4-flash`, and T2.129's author-vouched single-live-run caveat scales
with the corpus.

**Step 5 — Independent verdict** by a party that did not author the growth.

**Step 6 — Ledger and progress**, with §6's disclosure attached to the number.

## 6. The disclosure that travels with the result

Written before the number exists rather than after.

Clearing at ~500 makes the audit **decisive about a corpus its own measurer
wrote**: one authored dispute over ~500 authored corroborations. T2.130 already
recorded the limit — *a well-powered rule over authored evidence is a better
instrument measuring the same thing* — and this task sharpens the instrument
without lifting it.

**And the composition is more concentrated than v1 implied.** Diversity claims
cannot leave project `default` (§3.3), so at the recommended split `default`
holds **365 of 510** promoting claims (101 + 264 diversity, against 32 + 113
`none` in the spread projects). A future reader meeting "500 corroborations,
bound 0.00945, CLEARS" must meet, in the same breath, that every one was written
to be counted and that most sit in one project against one stand-in repository
fleet of at most six.

This is not an argument against the route. The owner chose it, a settled bound
beats an unsettled one. **The alternative is no longer an alternative: the owner
switched the scored rule to the Clopper-Pearson upper bound the same day
(T2.132, `949cd34d`), after this plan was written.** That strengthens this route
rather than competing with it — the targets below were *derived* from that bound
while the gate still scored the point estimate, so they were a self-imposed aim
and are now the gate's actual requirement. Reaching the window and clearing the
audit became one event instead of two. **Step 6 must quote this corrected form,
not the superseded sentence**, because this paragraph is the disclosure designed
to travel with the eventual number.
