# T3.03 — claim-level entailment pass + coverage routing

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: `superpowers:executing-plans`.
> Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax.

**Task:** `T3.03`, `Size: L`, `Model: Opus 5`, `Gate: G-E (label)`.
**Design:** doc 09 §4.3. **Runbook §3** forbids executing an `L` task directly,
which is why this exists.

**Revision 2, 2026-08-11 — REWORK applied.** Revision 1 came back **REWORK** at
Fable tier: spec-compliance NOT MET, three Critical, six Important. Every
load-bearing claim was re-verified in the tree before being applied. Three
things revision 1 would have shipped, marked so the next reader sees what was
nearly built:

- **The lead sentence has no citation slot** (`DraftArticle.lead: str`, no
  `fact_ids`), so under D1 it is a permanent uncited failure and coverage caps
  at N/(N+1). At the proposed 0.95 that means a *perfectly grounded* article
  needs ≥19 sentences to pass, against a 400-word budget yielding roughly
  16–26. Most articles would have routed to review **on their length, not
  their grounding**, every stub-judge test would still have passed, and the
  symptom would have surfaced only as G-E's distribution probe failing for no
  visible reason. See D8.
- **D5's provisional citation rows contradict the owning doc.** §4.3: "a
  **grounded** claim writes an `article_citations` row"; `fact_id` is `NOT
  NULL`, so the uncited claim D1 exists to count *cannot be represented*
  there. Ruled and replaced — see D5.
- **"Retry next run" is unimplementable, and the naive wiring destroys the
  article.** See D2 and D9.

**Goal.** Measure every synthesised article's claim-support coverage against
its cited facts, store that number as the article's `confidence`, and route the
ones below threshold to review instead of promoting them. Doc 09 §4.3 calls
this "the single most important mechanism in the doc: it is what makes an
article's badge honest."

**What T3.02 left for this task, exactly.** Articles exist, stored `candidate`
with `review_state='open'` and a **NULL** `confidence`, and the queue derives
`coverage_unmeasured` from that NULL. This task is what turns a NULL into a
number. Nothing else about the article changes.

## The premise that makes this safe to build now

`VerificationJudge.judge()` **refuses unconditionally** without a qualification
record (`JudgeNotQualifiedError`), scan-enforced as the single door. G-D is not
cleared, so no record exists, so this pass cannot produce a verdict today. That
is why the task text says "buildable now": the pass, its routing and its tests
all land, and what they cannot do is produce a *served grounded label*.

**So the pass must have a defined behaviour when the judge refuses**, and that
behaviour is not "coverage 0". See D2 — it is the decision most likely to be
got wrong, because a refusing judge and an unentailed claim both produce "no
entailment" and they mean opposite things.

## Decisions to make before coding, not during

**D1 — an uncited claim fails, and it fails without a model call.** Doc 09
§4.3: "a claim with no citation fails outright (no search-for-support fallback
— an uncited span is by definition ungrounded)". T3.02 already refuses a draft
containing an uncited sentence, so in practice every stored article's sentences
carry citations; this pass must still handle the case rather than assume it,
because an article stored before that refusal existed, or by a future writer,
would otherwise be scored against a claim count that silently excluded its
worst sentences.

**D2 — a refusing judge yields NO coverage, not zero coverage. (Core upheld;
two states added; the retry promise withdrawn.)** The two are
opposite claims: 0.0 means "measured, and nothing was entailed"; absent means
"not measured". Doc 09 §4.3 makes coverage the stored `confidence`, and T3.02
already fixed NULL as the honest spelling of unmeasured. So:

- `JudgeNotQualifiedError` → the run makes **no write at all** to that
  article's `confidence` or `status`, and records that the pass could not run.
  It must not write 0.0, must not open a review state that is already open, and
  must not journal a coverage measurement that did not happen.
- A per-claim judge *error* (timeout, vendor outage) is also not a failed
  claim. The article's coverage is unmeasurable this run; leave it NULL and
  retry next run. Counting an outage as an unentailed claim would drive an
  article below threshold for a reason that has nothing to do with its
  grounding.

Review upheld both the refusal rule and the whole-article rule. The
alternative to the second — scoring what *was* measured — changes the
denominator per run and lets a mostly-errored article promote on a 3-of-25
sample of itself, which is a false-promotion channel.

**Two response states revision 1 never classified**, both now explicit:

- `parse_verdict`'s **`valid=False`** — neither an exception nor a verdict. It
  counts as **unmeasured**, not unentailed. The qualification scorer's
  counts-as-a-miss rule is a *scoring convention* for measuring the judge; it
  is not evidence about the claim.
- **`coverage([])` is 0/0.** It must be `None` or a refusal, never a vacuous
  `1.0`. An article with no claims is not a perfectly grounded one.

A per-claim error also **aborts the remaining calls**: the article's outcome is
already fixed at unmeasured, so spending C−k more judge calls buys nothing.

**The "retry next run" promise is withdrawn**, because nothing implements it
and nothing can: once an article is stored, `select_for_synthesis` excludes its
subject until T3.05 re-synthesises. With G-D open — today's standing state —
**every** article lands unmeasured and stays so. That is recorded as a stated
consequence rather than papered over: the pre-qualification back-catalogue
reads `coverage_unmeasured` until T3.05, and T3.05 is handed the option of
treating "unmeasured" as a staleness reason.

**D3 — the threshold is a `config` row, it is G-E's bar, and stating it now is
lawful. (Corrected.)** Doc 09 §4.3: "an article below a `config` coverage
threshold does not ship as `corroborated`". Default **0.95**.

The gate ledger's G-E section says "Bar lives in: `config`" and its second
probe measures the coverage distribution above the runtime threshold — so this
row **is** G-E's bar. Stating an unstated bar *before* any data is not the
Tier-3 "a bar would have to change" stop; it is the repo's own discipline
(promotion bars committed before the first measurement, R-D's curve-before-
data), and no coverage number has ever been measured anywhere, so fitting is
impossible.

**The obligation revision 1 omitted:** the number and its derivation are
recorded in the gate ledger's G-E section and called out in the completion
report for owner ratification. A code default alone does not satisfy "the bar
lives in config".

Revision 1 called it "a floor … exactly as the verification ladder's thresholds
are floors" while building none of the ladder's clamp machinery. It is
**convention-recorded, not clamp-enforced**, and says so.

Quantisation, stated because it bites: at 0.95 any article under 20 claims
requires *every* claim entailed.

**D4 — what "ships as corroborated" means while G-D is open.** It means the
*stored* status may become `corroborated`; it does **not** mean a reader sees
one. T3.02 wired the article claim gate to the judge's record, so a promoted
article still serves as `candidate` with `groundedness_unmeasured` until G-D
clears. Both halves must be true and both must be tested, because they are
easy to conflate: this task may promote the column, and the serving boundary is
what keeps the label honest.

Note the ordering consequence: an article can only be promoted by a judge that
is qualified, and a qualified judge also opens the gate. So in practice the
promotion and the label arrive together — but they arrive through *different*
mechanisms, and a test that could not tell them apart would not notice if this
task started writing `corroborated` from an unqualified run.

**D5 — coverage is over claims, and the claim list comes from the draft in
memory. (Ruled.)** The
denominator is every claim in the stored article, including uncited ones (D1).
Deriving the claim list from the stored `content` re-parses prose this system
already had structured — T3.02 held `DraftSentence(text, fact_ids)` and threw
the structure away when it stored `draft.body`.

**Two options, and this plan picks the second:**

1. Re-split the stored content into sentences here. Cheap to write, and wrong
   in a way that compounds: a sentence splitter that disagrees with the model's
   own segmentation changes the denominator, so the same article scores
   differently depending on punctuation, and the citations cannot be matched
   back to spans at all.
2. **Persist the structure at synthesis time.** T3.02 stores the body; this
   task adds the per-sentence citations to `knowledge_articles.applies_when`'s
   sibling — a new JSONB column is a **baseline schema change**, which the
   repo's protocol allows (the schema is one unfrozen revision) but which
   serialises through one owner and must be argued.

**Ruled at review, and the answer is a third option revision 1 missed: nothing
needs recovering.** The pass runs in the same tick (D6), and
`synthesise_subject` still holds the `DraftArticle` in memory at that point —
so the pass takes the draft as an argument. Persistence was only ever needed
for the retry promise, which is itself withdrawn (D2).

**Provisional citation rows are rejected**, on three independent grounds:

1. Doc 09 §4.3 says "a **grounded** claim writes an `article_citations` row",
   and its flowchart records the citation only after the verdict; doc 04 §8.8
   defines `claim_span` as the span the fact **grounds**. Rows written before
   any verdict would make the provenance table carry unverified model
   proposals — which is exactly what `article_synthesis`'s own docstring says
   nothing may treat as grounding. Doing it anyway is a **Tier-3 amendment**.
2. `article_citations.fact_id` is `NOT NULL`, so the **uncited** claim D1
   exists to count cannot be represented there at all. Coverage read back from
   citation rows would silently exclude exactly those claims.
3. It is unnecessary, per the ruling above.

**What this task does write** is an `article_citations` row per **entailed**
claim — which §4.3 assigns to the pass explicitly. That does **not** invert
T3.03/T3.04: the FK already exists in the baseline, so T3.04 owns the
*enforcement and invalidation* behaviour ("an invalidated fact leaves the
citation intact and the article marked stale; a hard delete is impossible").
T3.02's plan said "`article_citations` rows — T3.04", so this reallocation is a
**Tier-2 amendment to log in the epic file**, not a silent move.

**D6 — where the pass runs.** Doc 09 §4.3: "entailment runs in the dream job
under doc 08's schedule umbrella with doc 11 §6's Tier-C concurrency bounds; it
never touches ingest or retrieval (invariant 15)." So it is part of the
`article_synthesis` tick, after the article is stored, not a new job — a second
job would need its own cursor over the same population and would double the
rotation state for one pass.

**D7 — cost is bounded, and the bound is Σ|fact_ids|, not C. (Corrected.)**
`DraftSentence.fact_ids` is a *list* and the prompt asks for "at least one", so
the pass costs one check per (claim, cited-fact) pair — worst case C×cap, not
C. Still bounded by construction, by the hub cap and the length budget.

**Serial per article, on one session.** `judge()` recomputes
`qualification_state` on the session it is handed, so concurrent `judge()`
calls on one session are illegal, and `qualification_probe` is scan-pinned to
the harness. Serial-per-article is the simplest topology that is bounded and
lawful; the per-article loop is where the bound already lives. No second
per-claim retry loop — it would multiply an already-bounded cost by an
unbounded factor.

**D8 — the lead cites facts too. (New — Critical.)** `DraftArticle.lead` has
no `fact_ids`, so under D1 it is uncited and fails permanently, capping every
article's coverage at N/(N+1). The two ways out are: exempt the lead from the
claim set, which amends §4.3's "checks every article sentence" and is a
**Tier-3 stop**; or give the lead a citation slot. **This plan takes the
second** — §4.2 already describes the call as emitting "prose with per-sentence
candidate fact ids attached", and a definitional lead about a subject is a
claim like any other. `article_synthesis.py`'s prompt, parse and `DraftArticle`
change accordingly, and its refusal for an uncited sentence extends to the
lead.

**D9 — the transaction boundary: commit the article, then measure.
(New — Critical.)** `JudgeNotQualifiedError` raised inside `synthesise_subject`
after `store_article` propagates to the driver's per-subject handler, and the
session exits **without commit — the article write itself rolls back**. So: the
article commits first, then the pass runs in a **fresh session**, with its own
writes (citations, confidence, status, review_state, journal) as one atomic
transaction. The session-lifetime invariant is satisfied because the first
transaction has committed before the second opens.

**D10 — an uncited claim blocks promotion outright. (New — Important.)** Done-
when #1 is "uncited sentences are impossible in a shipped article", and a
fractional threshold does not deliver it: at ≥20 claims one uncited claim still
clears 0.95 (23/24 = 0.958). Doc 04 §8.8 reads "uncited spans fail the
**article**" while §4.3 reads claim-level; the strict reconciliation — **any**
uncited claim blocks promotion regardless of coverage — makes the checkbox
literally true and is a tightening, lawful without amendment.

**D11 — the module layout is inverted by invariant 15. (New — Important.)**
`dream_jobs` imports `article_synthesis`, so `app.services.article_synthesis`
is a forbidden module for every non-umbrella file. `claim_entailment.py`
therefore **may not import** `DraftArticle`/`DraftSentence`. The tempting
"fix" — adding `claim_entailment` to the allowed set — would open an unpoliced
transitive route (hot path → claim_entailment → article_synthesis), because the
scan checks direct reach per file. So `claim_entailment` defines its own
`Claim` type and `article_synthesis` adapts and calls **downward** only.

**D12 — every transition journals. (New — Important.)** Doc 09: "**Every**
transition … journals an `events` row (`object_type = article`)" (convention
8). The `candidate → corroborated` promotion and the below-threshold hold both
journal; a refusal journals nothing, because no measurement happened.
`verified` + `article` are already in the closed vocabularies, so no widening
is needed. The machine's writer **refuses `confirmed`**, mirroring the fact
side. Whether the article transition adopts T2.113's strict-journal contract is
decided explicitly and recorded either way — the default here is **no**, since
G-E's probe reads the distribution rather than a per-transition denominator.

**D13 — multi-citation semantics. (New — Important.)** §4.3 phrases the check
singular ("claim *c* with cited fact *f*"); §4.2 lets the model propose
"fact(s)". The rule: ask per (claim, cited-fact) pair with early exit, and a
claim is grounded **iff any** cited fact entails it. A conjunction-grounded
sentence therefore fails toward review, which is the safe direction.

## Steps

- [ ] **Step 1 — the coverage calculation, pure.** `claim_entailment.py` with
      its own `Claim` type (D11). `coverage(verdicts) -> float | None`, `None`
      for unmeasured (D2). Tests: an uncited claim counts in the denominator
      and never in the numerator; all-entailed is 1.0; a single unmeasurable
      claim makes the whole article unmeasured; **`coverage([])` is `None`, not
      1.0** (the vacuity trap); a `valid=False` verdict is unmeasured, not
      unentailed.
- [ ] **Step 2 — the per-claim pass.** For each claim with a cited fact, ask
      `VerificationJudge.judge(premise=fact.fact, hypothesis=claim.text)`.
      Bounded concurrency (D7). Tests against a stubbed judge: every claim is
      asked exactly once; `JudgeNotQualifiedError` short-circuits the whole
      article (asking the rest cannot change the outcome); a per-claim error
      makes the article unmeasured, not unentailed.
- [ ] **Step 3 — the threshold and the routing.** `config` row (D3). At or
      above **and no uncited claim** (D10): status may become `corroborated`,
      `review_state` cleared. Below, or any uncited claim: status stays
      `candidate` and `review_state='open'`. Unmeasured: no write (D2). Each
      transition journals (D12). Tests: each of the four outcomes, the
      uncited-blocks-promotion case at a coverage that would otherwise clear,
      and a mutation drill on the comparison direction.
- [ ] **Step 4 — coverage is the stored confidence.** Write the number to
      `knowledge_articles.confidence`. Test: the queue's reason moves off
      `coverage_unmeasured` once measured, which is T3.02's arm proving it was
      derived rather than hardcoded.
- [ ] **Step 5 — the lead gets a citation slot, and entailed claims get
      rows.** D8: `article_synthesis`'s prompt, parse and `DraftArticle` give
      the lead `fact_ids`, and the uncited-sentence refusal covers it. D5: the
      pass writes one `article_citations` row per **entailed** claim, with its
      `claim_span` over the stored body. Tests: a lead with no citation is
      refused at synthesis; a citation row exists for an entailed claim and for
      no unentailed one.
- [ ] **Step 6 — wire into the tick, after the commit.** D9: the article
      commits, then the pass runs in a fresh session as one atomic
      transaction. Tests: a synthesis run measures coverage in the same tick;
      **a refusing judge leaves the article stored** — the regression this step
      exists to prevent is the raise rolling the article back — and unmeasured.
- [ ] **Step 7 — the serving boundary, re-proved.** A promoted article still
      serves `candidate` while G-D is open (D4), and the promotion and the
      label are separately observable.
- [ ] **Step 7b — the queue reason for a measured hold.** `_article_reason`
      returns `unspecified` for any non-NULL confidence, so a measured
      below-threshold article — the headline routing case — renders reasonless,
      re-creating the T2.112 defect. Add a `coverage_below_threshold` arm
      derived from the article's columns plus the threshold config row, read in
      `review_queue` and **never** by importing the pass (T2.112's lesson about
      dream-family imports on read surfaces).
- [ ] **Step 8 — the floor, and the ledger.** `./scripts/gate.sh`, plus D3's
      G-E bar recorded in the gate ledger with its derivation.
