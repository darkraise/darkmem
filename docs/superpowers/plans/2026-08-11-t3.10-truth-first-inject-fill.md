**Goal.** Turn a composed F3 selection into the `list[str]` that

Inject "adds a transport and a budget", not an order of its own (doc 15 §3.1);

## Decisions to make before coding, not during

**D1 — how a token is counted. (Corrected.)** There is no tokenizer in this repo
and adding one is a dependency decision, so the budget is an explicit
`estimate_tokens(text)` in the new module. Revision 1 proposed `ceil(len/4)`
documented as an *over-approximation*; that is **false and false in the
dangerous direction**. `len/4` is the average for English prose, while this
corpus's items are badge claims, entity names, code identifiers and dates —
punctuation- and digit-dense text real BPE tokenizers cut at 2.5–3.5
chars/token. A block would routinely exceed the budget it advertises, in a
feature whose whole point is not over-claiming. So:

- divisor **3**, not 4;
- the docstring says *heuristic that under-counts punctuation-dense text; the
  budget is exact only in estimator units* — never "over-approximation";
- the fill runs against `budget - HEADER_RESERVE`, a documented constant
  covering `render_block`'s header and the join newlines, which the fill itself
  never sees. This is the answer to "does splitting render from fill make the
  budget uncountable": the header is O(1), so a reserve is sufficient and no
  restructuring is needed. **`HEADER_RESERVE = 64`, and the fill also counts one
  char per admitted item for its join newline.** The re-review's arithmetic:
  the header is ~65–75 chars ≈ 22–25 estimator tokens at divisor 3, but the
  newlines scale with item count, so at 40–60 short items under a 2000 budget
  the real overhead is ~40–45. A single ~32 constant would have been quietly
  short.

**D2 — where the layer priority lives.** Unchanged; verified correct.
`LayerCompositionService` already orders by `(band, -tier, key)`. This task must
not re-rank *inside* a layer: it groups composed rows by layer and concatenates
`L2 → L3 → L0`, preserving composition order **within** each layer. Regrouping
by layer *is* a reorder of compose's interleaved order, and it is the one
reorder §3.4 owns — say so in the module docstring. A test asserts within-layer
order is untouched, because re-ranking here would be a second ranking authority
and §3.1 forbids exactly that.

**D3 — cold start. (Corrected — revision 1 was incoherent.)** Revision 1 said
the rule is decided "after the fill", which cannot work: candidates appended
after the budget is spent are over budget, and if the primary fill admitted
everything then candidates ride into every block and the cold-start branch is
unreachable, making its test vacuous. §3.4 is explicit — candidates are served
"**only** labelled **when nothing verified fits**". So:

- the primary fill admits an L2 row only when its **served** labels say verified
  (`labels["unverified"] is False`), plus L3 and L0 per §3.4;
- if zero verified items land, **re-run the fill once** with candidates admitted
  into the L2 slot, under the same budget;
- **disputed facts are unverified and therefore cold-start-only** — §3.4 does
  not settle it and this is the conservative reading, recorded here as a
  decision rather than left to the implementer.

**Operational consequence, stated so the drills are written honestly:** G-C is
open and its record unmintable, so every promoted rung serves as `candidate`.
Today nearly every block is therefore a **cold-start block**. That is the
serving boundary working, not a defect, and Step 5's mutation drills must not be
written assuming verified rows exist in production.

**D4 — truncation.** Unchanged; verified faithful to §3.4. An item is atomic: if
it does not fit, it is skipped and the fill **stops** rather than scanning on for
a smaller item. Continuing would silently reorder by size, a ranking this task
may not invent. The one exception is D6's safety exclusion, which is not
size-based.

**D5 — grouping. (New; revision 1 was silent.)** `ComposedRow.supported_by`
carries memories grouped under the fact they support, and
`LayerCompositionService.search` always passes `fact_sources`, so a nested
memory never also appears top-level — **there is no duplication bug**. But
silence would have meant nested rows are never rendered by accident. **Decision:
v1 does not render nested rows.** The fact's badge already states its origin
count, and the budget is better spent on distinct claims. This is a deliberate
deviation from §3.4's rendering sentence, recorded here, and one test pins it.

**D8 — a suppressed row has NO badge, and that is the gate working.
(New in revision 3; the scoped re-review found revision 2's three fixes
colliding on exactly the highest-stakes row.)** `row_labels` sets
`"badge": badge.as_dict() if badge is not None and suppression is None else
None` (`fact_badge.py:517`) — a withheld status must not carry a badge
asserting the corroboration it was withheld *for*. So on the flagship case —
raw `corroborated`, gate closed — `labels["badge"]` is `None`, and revision 2's
literal instructions would have an implementer crash there:
`labels["badge"]["claim"]` raises `TypeError`, and D-minor-3's "raise loudly if
a badge-bearing row carries no badge" would fire on a legitimately suppressed
row. Therefore:

- **A suppressed row renders its served status and its `label_suppressed`
  reason, and no claim sentence.** There is no claim to render; inventing one
  is the exact over-claim the suppression exists to prevent.
- **The no-badge raise keys on the `badge` key being *absent*** — the
  `compose(badges=None)` path, a caller that never assembled badges — **never on
  present-and-`None`**, which is the suppression path. Collapsing the two would
  make the gate working indistinguishable from a bug.
- The three Step 1 tests (suppressed row renders; every emitted item carries its
  badge claim; no-badge raises) are only mutually satisfiable under this rule,
  so the "carries its badge claim verbatim" test is scoped to rows that
  **have** one.

**D6 — a marker-bearing item must not take down the inject. (New.)**
`render_block` raises `ManagedBlockCorrupted` on any item containing
`<!-- darkmem:begin/end -->`. This repo's own docs contain those literals and
darkmem stores memories about darkmem, so a summary quoting T3.09's markers is
entirely plausible — and one such row would fail **every** session-start inject
until it rotated out of the selection. The fill is the only layer that can
degrade gracefully: it **drops** such an item, counts the drop, and a test pins
it. This is a safety exclusion, not the size-based reordering D4 forbids.

**D7 — the budget has one authority, and it already exists. (Corrected —
revision 1 would have minted a second.)** `distribution_policy.token_budget`
(`config` row, default 2000) shipped with the distribution surface and is what
`GET /api/v1/distribution` shows as the policy in force. A new
`inject_token_budget` in `app/config.py` would give the operator a screen that
edits one budget while the fill obeys another. The fill takes the budget as a
**parameter** (it must, to stay pure); the caller reads `distribution_policy`.
Revision 1's claim that a new row would match "the way the other inject rows are
read" was simply wrong: `inject_adapters` and `distribution_policy` are `config`
**table** rows, not `app/config.py` entries.
