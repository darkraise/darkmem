# Design — the round-5 premise exclusion: entailment is a determined verdict

**Status:** design, 2026-08-14. Written after step 9's blind adjudication of the
round-4 shared-premise population
(`backend/scripts/benchlib/entailment/dispatch/2026-08-14-t2124-step9/`), for the
frame fix the owner-decision packet names option B: fix the generator before
round 5, never patch the round-4 census under it.
**What this document is not:** it does not touch the ratified round-4 frame,
matrix, assignment or census (sitting 2, 2026-08-13, T = 280,
contradicted 80 / entailed 110 / unsupported 90); it does not decide the pending
owner questions (the step-9 gate count, the six contested cases, the A/B/C
disposition of the round-4 census); and it authors no pair, premise or label.
**Screen status:** this file is owner/controller material, not blind-party
material — it cites the step-9 adjudication counts and census arithmetic, which
are the sanctioned residual categories; no measured judge result appears here.

## 1. The defect, verified against the code and the data

Round 4's assignment (`entailment_assignment.build_round4_assignment`) excludes
premise reuse through `ROUND4_DETERMINED_SLICES = {"direction", "verbatim"}`,
keyed **per slice**: a premise already holding a slot *of that slice* is
ineligible for another slot *of that slice*. Everywhere else a shared
`(premise, verdict)` slot pair is permitted and stamped `shared_note`
("build your hypothesis on a different element … than the most direct
construction") — an authoring instruction, not an exclusion.

Re-derived from the committed inputs on 2026-08-14 (the assignment is
test-pinned deterministic, so this is the assignment the authors received):

- The derived round-4 assignment hands **17 premises two or more entailed
  slots** (entailed manifest slots: verbatim 27 + paraphrase 41 +
  weakening 34 = 102).
- The 8 unanimously adjudicated duplicates sit on 8 of those 17 premises —
  every one of them. Their slice pairs: verbatim×paraphrase ×4,
  paraphrase×paraphrase ×2, weakening×weakening ×1, weakening×paraphrase ×1.
- So the per-slice keying failed twice over: cross-slice collisions
  (verbatim×paraphrase — the premise *was* in a determined slice, but the
  exclusion could not see across the slice boundary) and within-slice
  collisions in slices never listed (paraphrase, weakening).
- The class asymmetry is total and matches the packet's characterisation:
  16 of 24 shared-`(premise, verdict)` candidate groups are entailed, and all
  14 duplicate-or-contested findings are entailed; the 7 unsupported and
  1 contradicted groups adjudicated unanimously distinct.
- Corroboration from the authoring record itself
  (`dispatch/2026-08-14-t2124/00-README.md`): two authors *skipped* direction
  slots under a `shared_note` because "the differentiation the note requires is
  impossible" for a determined hypothesis. The entailed class had the same
  impossibility and no name for it, so its authors complied instead of
  skipping — and converged.

The root cause, stated once: **determination is a property of the verdict, not
of the slice.** An entailed hypothesis must be licensed by its premise, and a
premise licenses a narrow set of claims — restatement (verbatim), meaning-
preserving rewording (paraphrase), and the small lattice of weakenings — so two
blind authors given one premise and the verdict `entailed` converge on one
proposition. `contradicted` and `unsupported` hypotheses live in open spaces (a
premise can be contradicted or left unsupported in unboundedly many ways), which
is why the `shared_note` instruction is satisfiable there and the measured
duplicate count is zero. Round 4 named two symptoms of determination
("the hypothesis *is* the reversal", "near-literal restatement") and missed the
class that generates both.

## 2. The rule (R5), precisely, with zero free parameters

> **R5 — entailed-determined exclusion.** In the round-5 assignment, a premise
> (`triple_key`) may fund **at most one slot whose determined verdict is
> `entailed`**, across all slices. A slot's determined verdict is `entailed`
> exactly when its slice's lawful verdict set under `SLICE_VERDICTS` is exactly
> `{"entailed"}` (today: `verbatim`, `paraphrase`, `weakening` — derived from
> the committed table, never listed by hand). The `direction` per-slice
> exclusion stands unchanged. `verbatim`'s old per-slice rule is strictly
> subsumed by R5 and is not separately stated.

Supporting clauses, each mechanical:

- **Fail-loud guard.** If any slice drawing manifest premises carries
  `entailed` in a lawful verdict set that is not exactly `{"entailed"}`, the
  builder raises instead of assigning. Today that is unrepresentable —
  `temporal` is the only mixed entailed-bearing slice and takes no manifest
  premise under doc 17 §11's exemption — so the guard exists for the day either
  the exemption ends or the taxonomy gains a mixed entailed-bearing slice, when
  silent under-exclusion is exactly the recurrence this design closes.
- **Hand-authored premises carry the same rule as an authoring instruction**:
  a hand-authored premise (temporal's, and any skip re-author's) takes at most
  one entailed pair. Enforcement is not the instruction but the round-5 step-9
  gate below.
- **The round-5 step-9 gate for the entailed class becomes structural**: the
  candidate population "live pairs sharing a byte-identical premise and verdict
  `entailed`" must be **empty** — a mechanical count, needing no blind
  adjudication, because R5 makes the assigned-premise portion unrepresentable
  and the hand-authored portion violates a stated rule rather than an
  unsatisfiable instruction. The contradicted and unsupported candidate
  populations keep the step-9 blind-adjudication shape (measured clean this
  round, not structurally clean).
- **`shared_note` survives only where it is satisfiable**: it continues to
  annotate shared C/U slots; no entailed slot can carry one because no entailed
  slot shares a premise.

## 3. Why exclusion, and not the alternatives

**A per-slice (or per-verdict) cap on shared premises** is either vacuous or
identical to R5: every one of the 8 duplicates occurred at exactly two shared
uses, the minimum any cap ≥ 2 permits, so the only cap that closes the defect
is 1 — which is the exclusion.

**A verdict-aware differentiation constraint** (mandate that two entailed
hypotheses on one premise generalise *opposite* slots, the shape of the owner's
two lawful rulings) is rejected on three grounds. It depends on the pending
owner ruling that this design is barred from assuming — if the six contested
cases are duplicates under the opposite-slot reading, one-way generalisation is
not a lawful differentiation and the constraint's premise collapses. It is
enforced at authoring and adjudication rather than at the generator, and round 4
measured exactly what authoring instructions are worth on a determined class:
the `shared_note` was present on every colliding slot and the duplicates
happened anyway. And even enforced perfectly it stamps a recognisable template
(one subject-generalisation, one object-generalisation per shared premise) onto
the census — a structural fingerprint where the frame's whole discipline is that
composition carries no signal.

**Post-hoc screening and re-authoring** (the packet's option A) treats
instances under an unfixed generator: the assignment hands the same collisions
out again and re-authoring re-rolls loaded dice. The packet already rejects it;
this design does too.

**The stronger variant — exclusion for every verdict, not only entailed** —
was considered and set aside. It would make the whole step-9 candidate
population empty by construction, which is attractive. But the measured defect
is zero outside the entailed class (0 of 7 unsupported, 0 of 1 contradicted
groups), the differentiation instruction is demonstrably satisfiable there, and
universal exclusion spends harvest capacity (distinct premises for all 239
manifest slots per verdict row) against a defect measured absent — raising the
corpus-growth floor for nothing the adjudication found. If a later round
measures a C/U duplicate, the R5 mechanism generalises by widening the derived
determined-verdict set, through the same ratification channel.

## 4. Feasibility, measured on the committed round-4 inputs

R5 does not move the matrix. Checked against the ratified frame and the
committed harvest (arithmetic re-runnable by anyone):

- Entailed manifest slots: 27 + 41 + 34 = **102**, each needing a distinct
  premise under R5.
- Fresh facts after the round-4 exclusion: **163** ≥ 102. With rule 3's 25%
  skip margin applied to the entailed row: ceil(102 × 5/4) = **128** ≤ 163.
- Direction's class quotas are unaffected: 62 reversal-exclusive facts ≥ 26
  contradicted slots; 98 non-exclusive ≥ 8 unsupported slots.
- Total capacity: the 137 non-entailed manifest slots draw from
  102 × 3 + 61 × 4 = 550 remaining premise-uses at cap 4.

So the counterfactual "round 4 under R5" assigns completely — the inert
implementation's tests build it and assert so. A round-5 harvest must clear the
same bar, which is §5's rule-3 amendment.

## 5. What the round-5 frame derivation must add (rule-3 amendment)

Rule 3 of the ratified derivation currently gates feasibility on
`F ≥ ceil(ceil(ceil(T/2) / cap) × 5/4)` — premise-uses, blind to distinctness.
R5 adds a second, row-level constraint:

> **F ≥ ceil(E_manifest × 5/4)**, where `E_manifest` is the sum of the derived
> matrix's entailed cells over slices drawing manifest premises (today:
> E minus temporal's entailed cell).

The constraint is evaluated after rules 1–2 produce the candidate matrix at
each T (the current script checks capacity before deriving cells; the round-5
script must derive first, gate second). Like the existing capacity gate it can
only **stop** the round — it is monotonically harder as T grows, so it can
never move T upward off the minimum, preserving B3's argument that the rule
emits its minimum T for every harvest the round survives.

## 6. What lands now, inertly, and what does not

**Lands now** (this change, no ratified artifact touched):

- `entailment_assignment.py` gains a round-5 section: `entailed_determined_slices()`
  (derived from `SLICE_VERDICTS`), `ROUND5_FRAME_PATH`, `round5_seed`, and
  `build_round5_assignment` implementing R5 with the fail-loud guard. It is
  inert by construction: its default frame and exclusion-list paths
  (`entailment/round5_frame.json`, `entailment/round5_excluded_triple_keys.json`
  — both round-5 chartering artifacts) do not exist and nothing calls the
  builder; the round-4 builder and its pinned assignment are byte-untouched.
- Tests (`tests/test_round5_assignment.py`): the determined set derives from
  `SLICE_VERDICTS`; the builder is deterministic and frame-conformant on the
  round-4 inputs (the counterfactual); **the predicate bites** — the round-4
  assignment hands two entailed slots to each of the 8 confirmed duplicates'
  premises and the R5 counterfactual hands at most one entailed slot to any
  premise, so a slot of every confirmed duplicate would not have been assigned;
  the guard raises on a mixed entailed-bearing slice; and the default path is
  asserted absent, so the round-5 frame landing is a visible diff here.

**Does not land now:** the round-5 frame script (its rule needs sitting-1
ratification first, §7), any regeneration of the round-4 assignment, any edit
to the ratified matrix or the census, any change to the step-9 runner's gate
(the round-4 census's gate question is the owner's, pending), and any gold
content.

## 7. Re-ratification R5 triggers, exactly

1. **The exclusion rule is a rule amendment → sitting-1 channel.** Plan §7
   step 5's assignment discipline ("excludes, for any slice whose hypothesis
   the premise determines, any premise already assigned a live pair of that
   slice") is part of what sitting 1 ratified; R5 replaces its slice-scoped
   form with the verdict-scoped form for the entailed class. Plan §5 fixes the
   channel: frame changes loop "to a rule amendment through sitting 1's
   channel — never to a hand edit". The owner ratifies R5 (and §5's rule-3
   amendment, which is part of the same ratified derivation text) before any
   round-5 pair is authored.
2. **The emitted round-5 frame and assignment take a sitting-2-style bounded
   ratification** — byte-regeneration by an independent reviewer, mechanical
   checks green, matrix equals the ratified rule's output — exactly as round 4's
   did. A new frame file (`round5_frame.json`), never an edit of the ratified
   `round4_frame.json`.
3. **Nothing about round 4 is re-ratified.** The sitting-2 matrix, the census
   and the committed assignment stand; R5 changes which premise funds which
   slot in a *future* assignment, never a count. The disposition of the
   round-4 census (A/B/C, and the six contested cases) is the owner's open
   decision from the step-9 packet and is neither taken nor prejudged here.

## 8. Where this design depends on pending owner rulings — and where it does not

R5's validity rests on the **8 unanimous** duplicates alone: 8 of 16 entailed
shared-premise groups collapsing under two blind adjudicators who recovered all
five controls justifies excluding the collision shape regardless of how the six
contested cases are ruled. The contested ruling moves only (a) the recorded
tally (8 vs 14) and the strength of §1's "close to unsatisfiable" claim, and
(b) the round-4 census's disposition. Under R5 the contested *shape* — two
entailed hypotheses on one premise, one generalising one slot one-way — is
unrepresentable among assigned slots, so round-5 generation needs no answer to
it. The one considered alternative that does depend on the ruling (the
differentiation constraint, §3) is rejected partly for that dependence.
