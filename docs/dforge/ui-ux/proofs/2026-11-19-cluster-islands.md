**UX-1 details:**
- **Owner ruling (2026-11-19):** the first build packed the islands round a centre island and stretched the others along the radius toward it, a sunburst. Asked to accept it, fix only the stretch, or fix both, the owner chose "Fix both (Recommended)": a wide archipelago, "no hub, no spokes". The spec's Decisions and Architecture now say so, and A14 and A15 are its acceptance statements.
- **The stretch had a measured cause.** On the full fixture, with the renderer's ForceAtlas2 settings, an island simulated together with the others ended 2 to 15 times longer than wide (mean 5.2) with its long axis pointing at the centre (mean |cos| 0.99); the same island simulated alone was near-round (mean 1.1) with no radial alignment (0.61). ForceAtlas2 repels every pair of nodes, so neighbouring islands squeezed each other. `simulationCentres` now runs each island at its own far-apart spot in the simulation, and every frame still refits it onto its drawn centre. The A15 test shows the result: mean elongation 1.23 shared against 1.24 alone, worst island 1.08 times its alone value.
- **The silhouette.** `placeIslands` no longer has a spiral start or a pull toward a centre. Islands start on a serpentine grid inside a 16:10 rectangle, ordered so linked islands are neighbours, with a fixed jitter per island; linked islands pull together, collisions keep the gap, a containment force holds them inside, and the short axis is then stretched until the bounding box is 16:10. The A14 test reads a bounding aspect of 1.60 to 1.62 on all four layouts (before: 0.87 to 1.01). A3 (more shared edges, closer) still passes.
- **What the pictures show.** All board and live All (4,759 memories): about fifteen islands spread across the frame in loose rows, no island in a hub position, each round-to-irregular in its own shape. Default 1,000 board and live page: irregular islands with visible sub-groups, spread wide. Scale20k (60 equal islands, no links between them): nothing moves the islands off their seed cells, so it reads as loose, jittered rows. It is a rectangle, not a disc, and it is the synthetic worst case.
- **A side effect, fixed.** The rectangle fills the frame's corners, where the toolbar buttons sit up to 44 px below the canvas top, and one live capture had an island just under them. The memory canvas's `stagePadding` went from 40 to 64 px; at the fitted view every re-captured live state (both viewports and themes) keeps every island clear of the toolbar, read off a strip of each capture's top 144 px. The zoomed-in regions states run under it, as any zoomed view does.

**UX-4 details:**
- **A judge pass failed this row; what it was.** Turning the entity layer on re-ran the force layout, so memories moved: entity ids were part of the renderer's layout signature, which was right when entities joined the simulation and wrong since they ride on their memories' centroid. The signature now counts memories only. A new test mounts the renderer with the layer off, lets the layout settle, turns the layer on and checks that no layout run starts and that no memory moves by more than 0.005 graph units. That is the drift of the one refit and spread pass every rebuild makes, under a hundredth of a pixel. Without the fix the test fails with two layout runs instead of one.
- **Live, same page.** Each `live-entity-layer-before-*` capture is taken on the settled page just before the switch is pressed, and `live-entity-layer-*` 15 s after. Islands keep their places and shapes; diamonds appear among their memories and in the gaps between islands. `entity-layer-overlap.txt` measures it: the after-capture best matches the before-capture at an offset of at most 2 px (dark 1280x800 (0,1), dark 1920x1080 (-2,1), light 1280x800 (-1,0), light 1920x1080 (-1,2)), and shifting it 3 px sideways or 6 px diagonally loses about half the overlap. The 1 to 2 px is the whole frame refitting to a bounding box the diamonds widen, not memories moving: the test above reads graph coordinates, not pixels. About 15 to 30% of memory pixels change colour at the best offset because diamonds and their borders are drawn over them.
- The earlier captures compared two separate page loads (default against entity layer), whose force runs differ; the same-page pair replaces them as evidence.

**UI-1 details:**
- **Switch:** Settings → Encoding shows "Regions" as a switch (accessible name "Cluster regions"), off by default, in place of the segment control. `getByRole("radiogroup", { name: "Regions" })` finds none on the live page (`legacyRadiogroup: 0`).
- **Keyboard:** with the switch focused, Space turns it on (`aria-checked` false → true), Space again turns it off, and Space once more then "Reset graph view" returns it to false, in both themes (`keys-dark.json`, `keys-light.json`).
- **Off and Reset draw no region.** After the switch goes from on to off (`keys-switch-off-after-on-*`) and after Reset (`keys-after-reset-*`) the canvas shows no outline or fill, in both themes. `keys.mjs` captures only once the hull canvas holds 0 drawn pixels. Its recorded "settled" times start after fixed sleeps (800 ms before the off poll, 1,500 ms before the Reset poll), so they are not latencies and are not quoted here.
- **A first judge pass failed this row; what it was.** An earlier capture of the same sequence, taken 2 s after Reset, still showed every region while the switch read off. The canvas does clear: every later probe read 0 drawn pixels. The delay is not in the region drawing. `scripts/stall.mjs`, timed from the key press, shows the switch's own `aria-checked` flipping at the same moment as the canvas (1,280 ms and 697 ms after a 12 s settle; 2,156 ms and 2,097 ms after a 60 s settle), and the page's long tasks over that span were 94 to 239 ms. A control with no graph code, the settings trigger button, took 1,332 ms for its first press. Later toggles took 5 to 500 ms (`scripts/latency.mjs`, `stall.mjs`). The host had 16 cores and a load average of 13 to 22 from another project's test run. So the first interaction after load is slow page-wide on this host, for a reason not isolated here (CPU contention and first-interaction warm-up are both consistent with it). The first claim in an earlier draft, that a software-GL repaint was slow, is withdrawn: the renderer redraws synchronously from `refresh()`. Not measured: a GPU, a second viewport, or the light theme. The pure decision the redraw depends on, "mode off yields no groups and no shapes", had no test; `framed-hull.test.ts` now has one.
